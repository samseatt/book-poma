## Reader value

The chapter now gives an educated reader a usable account of how neural activity is transmitted, altered by experience and consequences, organized into several forms of memory, and recruited for prediction and making. It explains mechanisms beyond a catalogue of names while keeping consciousness genuinely unsettled. Its biological-network account prepares the architecture mirror in Interlude 3 and later ML discussion without trying to supply either in full.

This is an editorial judgment, not evidence from reader testing. Detailed neuroanatomy, clinical neurology and comprehensive consciousness theory are outside its allotted role. The addition increases the incoming main text modestly, chiefly for mechanism, memory and evidentiary precision.

## Still open for the author

Alice is deliberately unfinished intellectual work. Its most productive next step is to say what the proposed coupling accounts for that a sophisticated unconscious control loop does not, and what evidence could discriminate the two. Neural resonance, recurrence and complexity provide candidates, not a demonstrated solution to subjective experience.

The lattice/inflation analogy remains useful as a comparison of reusable structure. Cortical heterogeneity prevents it from becoming a literal semiconductor model. Nothing here establishes the broader possibility-space proposal in Chapter 1.

The personal anecdotes, weather and remembered speech remain author-supplied evidence. Public science and historical sources do not independently authenticate them. No request to recertify the memories is implied.
