*The twist, in one line: money compresses unlike values into transferable possibility; accumulated, it can acquire the power to reshape everyone else's possibilities. Can we keep the gravity that gathers useful work without surrendering to the gravity that captures it?*

Alice's croquet game had flamingoes for mallets, hedgehogs for balls and soldiers who kept getting up from their jobs as hoops. The Queen supplied the remaining uncertainty.[^v1-c7-carroll] It is difficult to improve your stroke when the equipment has other appointments. Economics at least gives us a number. We can keep score while the lawn moves.

Money's convenience is that the number travels without most of its history. The amount can survive after the work, the damage and the luck behind it have disappeared from view. It can be added to other amounts, invested, inherited and turned into claims on more of the world. Here my pile of "money" includes those claims: shares, land, lending power, the things a large fortune holds instead of cash. The question is what happens when this portable possibility gathers weight.

At first, more money gives its holder more choices. But follow the gathering further. A supplier becomes dependent on one buyer. A town's livelihoods turn around one employer. Competitors must pass through an infrastructure their competitor owns. Each person can still choose, yet the costs of their choices have changed around them. The pile has begun to alter the routes available to people who never agreed to join it.

I want to try the gravitational picture here. The pile changes the economic geometry: which opportunities are near, which are prohibitively far, and which journeys require passage through somebody else's property. "Distance" would mean the cost, time, risk and dependence involved in reaching an opportunity.[^v1-c7-gravity] The hand still works. It works on a surface someone else has bent.

We do need economic solar systems. A fabrication plant cannot be assembled from eleven billion independent decisions about where to put the sink. Large projects need resources held together long enough to accomplish something. But gathering the work and owning its whole neighborhood are different achievements. A center can make possibilities reachable for others; it can also make itself unavoidable. Perhaps the important measure of a pile is how much useful coordination it enables compared with how much dependence it creates.

That makes the conjecture more demanding than a complaint about rich people. Imagine two economies with similar output and equally unequal fortunes. In one, people can change suppliers, carry their records elsewhere and organize a competing service. In the other, every useful exit crosses the same owner's bridge. The usual totals could look much alike. The lives available within them would not. Could we measure that difference as a kind of curvature, and learn which forms of accumulation open the surrounding possibility space and which close it? The old amount would remain useful. It would stop being sufficient.

Money has always bought time: reserves let one person wait while another must sell; debt commits future income before it arrives. Agents could extend the advantage into the futures considered before a decision. A system might rehearse thousands of supply disruptions, bids and responses while its counterpart is still opening a spreadsheet. This is a kind of *time-inflation*: more possible courses explored within the same interval, with the advantage accruing to whoever can act on the exploration. It does not require a correct prophecy every time. A sufficiently powerful owner can also act to make a favored forecast come true. Foresight becomes one of the means of shaping the field it surveys.

There is another direction that intelligence could take. The invisible hand has always employed rather a lot of visible people. We compare prices, read reviews, try the shoes, send them back and write a review for the next shopper: unpaid testers for a market whose sensing depends on us. Chapter 6's farmer domesticated the field and then had to report to it. The shopper domesticated the market and reports to it daily. Must better coordination always arrive as more homework for the people with the least time to spare?

By *smart money*, I mean money with memory and agency around it: a system that can follow the relations a price leaves out, anticipate a need, compare ways of meeting it and help arrange the work. The unit used to settle a payment need not carry the whole account. A graph of the transactions could retain what was taken, what was repaired, whose labor contributed and which obligations remain, with the connections still attached. Like the many coordinates of an embedding, these could preserve distinctions that a single balance erases. Remembering would have to be selective; buying apples should not require handing over your life story.

More coordinates alone would not make it fair. Whoever chooses them, controls the agents or owns their records could acquire a larger pile of a new kind. Compress everything back into one unquestionable score and we have furnished the Queen with a better calculator. The harder possibility is coordination in which different values remain distinguishable and contestable, while enough is shared to let strangers accomplish something together. An agent representing the river would need consequences to follow its evidence; being permitted to submit a report is not much of a bank account.

I would like to know whether such a system could gather the resources for a great undertaking without letting the gathering quietly acquire the power to price everyone else's escape. That is the wager behind money-gravity: measure the altered paths, not only the pile, and use the richer memory to change what accumulation is allowed to do. Whether we can build it without another Queen taking charge belongs to Volume III.
