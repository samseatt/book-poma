# Preserved author inquiry: short paths, possibility and internal worlds

23 October 2026 · Research agenda, not settled science or manuscript insert.

The author proposes a race through possibility: with very many entrants in a 5 km race and a 100-mile race, the earliest arrivals should overwhelmingly come from the shorter route; a 5 km versus 6 km comparison should be less extreme. The intended connection is that cheap paths dominate the structures we encounter, from a highly uniform possibility substrate to biological search. He wants to explore a possible connection with the central limit theorem and the prevalence of normal distributions. This is his explanatory ambition, not a claim that Interlude 2 has demonstrated it.

To turn the race into a model, specify how many runners enter each route, when they start, their speed distributions, the selection rule for observations and what counts as a route. Near-infinity supplies none of those measures by itself. Under equal starts and bounded speeds the short race can dominate early completion; changed entry proportions, extreme speeds or a different stopping rule can change that. The analogy concerns completion-time or search-cost bias. It is not automatically the same as Occam’s razor, a Bayesian Occam factor, a shortest-description prior or a physical selection law.

Solomonoff gives one useful formal neighbor: prediction by weighted computable descriptions. Runtime and description length are different; a short program can run for an enormous time. A computational-search version of the author’s idea would need to specify both costs. Possible later leads include resource-bounded algorithmic probability and Levin-style search, to be researched before attribution.

The central limit theorem is not an unexplained rule that all distributions are Gaussian. Particular normalized sums approach a normal distribution under stated conditions; dependence, heavy tails, multiplicative processes or selection can produce other limits. The author’s early-finishers example concerns order statistics/first passage, which is a different mathematical problem. A valuable research test would ask which aggregate or selection operation could connect them, rather than starting by assuming the connection.

The author’s stronger possibility conjecture is that sufficiently stable, mutually related possibilities would be experienced from within as reality. Preserve its challenge to an unexamined outside view. Existence or experience does not establish a historical transition from absolute nothing; “something from nothing happened for sure” remains the author’s proposed framing, not an empirical finding certified in this pass. Uniformity alone also does not supply a probability measure, dynamics or an observer. These are places to make the challenge sharper, not reasons to replace it with conventional physics by fiat.

Bob’s local, defensible foothold is a learned representation of a data-generating world: the Othello model learned board state from move sequences, and interventions on that representation affected output. Distinguish representation, simulation, operational access to a world, subjective experience and ontological reality. One does not establish all the others. The manuscript preserves the question of whether relations could constitute an internal reality, while leaving the additional step visible.

Recovered incoming wording, retained only as research provenance: “the universes, genomes and theories that win are the ones with the shortest recipe”; “an early accident, carved over eons by Occam’s chisel toward something close to optimal.” These formulations have been removed from the factual account because neither algorithmic complexity nor the cited genetic-code experiment supports them. The author may return to the underlying idea with a specified model and explicit conjectural status.
