Your strongest proposal is that a sufficiently structured system of relations could constitute a physical world for its own inhabitants without a second act of material installation. A separate proposal says that economical structures should carry greater weight. Keeping these claims distinguishable makes both more interesting: the first concerns what existence is; the second concerns why we encounter this kind of existence.

There are substantial precedents and useful mathematical connections. None of the sources below establishes the combined account. The most promising work concerns algorithmic probability, quantum reconstruction, emergent geometry, thermal time and universality. They supply different pieces, under different assumptions; joining their names does not join their equations.

## A proposed articulation of the conjecture

Before putting energy into the universe, I want to ask what we think we have put in. Energy already belongs to a description of relationships and change. Invoking a primordial supply may move the question upstream, but the invoice still arrives.

My working picture begins with possibility: configurations that could hold together, relations through which their parts constrain one another, and arrangements of those relations capable of supporting further arrangements. I do not mean possibilities floating in an enormous room. The room, its distances and even the order in which anything happens are among the things to account for. At this level, “interaction” initially means compatibility and mutual constraint. Calling it motion would give time an unauthorized head start.

The conjecture is that some sufficiently rich relational structures need no additional ingredient to become real *for what belongs to them*. An inhabitant would be a pattern within the same structure as its surroundings. Its instruments, memories and resistance to those surroundings would participate in the relations being measured. There would be no separate observer waiting outside the possibilities for a universe to become solid enough to enter. Solidity itself would have to be an internally intelligible relationship.

This does not require a mind to make a world. Human experience would be a later, elaborate achievement within it. Much simpler systems could constrain one another and carry records without feeling anything. What I am asking is whether their mutual constraints already supply the physical content for which we keep demanding an additional substance.

I suspect that economy matters. Repeating a rule can describe an immense structure without specifying every component individually. But a short description, a common occurrence and a small energy bill are three different discounts. My proposed *relative conformational bias* still owes us an account of how they might be connected. I cannot make simplicity a cosmic force merely by admiring it.

If this approach works, physics would describe the organization that such inhabitants encounter from within. Its equations would remain indispensable: they tell us precisely what any proposed foundation must recover. The conjecture asks whether those regularities require an external lawgiver, a simulator or an independently supplied substance. It also leaves a deeper question standing: why should there be any possibilities or relations at all? Removing the construction crew is progress only if we have not hidden it in the word “possible.”

## What possibility space and Hilbert space each mean

“Possibility space” needs a declared level. Without that, the argument can change its subject while appearing to move forward.

| Level | What is being specified | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logical or modal possibilities | What could be the case under a stated conception of consistency | Whether it is mathematically realizable, physically allowed or actual |
| A mathematical state or configuration space | Allowed configurations of a chosen model, with specified structure | Which configurations occur and how they are weighted or related dynamically |
| A probability model | Outcomes or events, a measure and conditioning rules | Whether these probabilities describe evidence, physical chances or both |
| Quantum state space | Quantum states and the mathematical structure needed to compare, combine and measure them | Interpretation, dynamics, physical subsystem structure and applicability |
| Your proposed prephysical possibility | Relations from which physical distinctions might themselves arise | What the primitive relations are, what selects or weights them, and whether they recover physics |

These are distinctions of explanatory role, not stages through which a universe necessarily passes.

Combining state spaces also requires a choice: a Cartesian product gives a classical joint configuration, while a tensor product serves the usual quantum composition. Neither operation alone specifies an interaction. And possibilities *within one theory* differ from possible theories with different laws. The conjecture should say when it crosses that boundary; there is no automatically supplied geometry or measure on an unrestricted collection of theories.

A Hilbert space is a complete inner-product vector space; ordinary quantum theory uses complex Hilbert spaces. A pure state is represented by a ray, with general states represented by density operators. The inner product and measurement rules do work that a bare inventory of possibilities cannot do. In a chosen composite-system description, tensor products allow entangled states.[^poss-preskill]

For a simple illustration, the states

\[
|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2,
\qquad
|-\rangle=(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt2
\]

both give equal probabilities for 0 and 1 in that measurement basis. Yet an appropriate different measurement distinguishes them perfectly. A list of those two outcome probabilities loses the relative phase. Thus “quantum possibility” carries relationships absent from ordinary alternatives; enlarging a list to infinity does not supply them.

Hilbert space could conceivably be a representation recovered from a deeper account. Chiribella, D’Ariano and Perinotti reconstruct quantum theory from operational principles, including a crucial purification postulate. Their result demonstrates the value of asking why this mathematical structure is appropriate. It does not derive it from unrestricted possibility.[^poss-reconstruction]

Neither high-dimensional Hilbert space nor an imagined manifold is automatically physical space. “Manifold” already specifies mathematical organization. A Calabi–Yau manifold is a restricted kind of geometry, not a name for everything conceivable. Likewise, immense finite size and mathematical infinity are different ideas; neither ensures that an imagined object is consistent. For the earliest premise, **possible relational structures** is cleaner than an already geometric, infinite-dimensional cosmic container.

## The strongest research connections

### Structure as reality and the internal observer

Tegmark’s mathematical-universe hypothesis is a close ontological precedent: physical reality is proposed to be mathematical structure. It does not require mathematics to happen earlier in time, or a computer to execute the universe. Your account can remain more cautious about identifying every mathematical structure with physical existence.[^poss-tegmark]

Müller supplies an especially useful comparison to your inside-view reasoning. His *Law without law* starts with observer states and an algorithmic-probability postulate; within that framework, apparent external worlds with simple computable laws emerge statistically. This is an explicitly incomplete proposal, and its observer-first starting point differs from your attempt to include nonconscious physical relations from the outset.[^poss-muller]

The consequence for your argument is conceptual: demanding a second material realization is not a neutral objection if the proposal identifies physical existence with a kind of relational structure. But that identification remains a substantive hypothesis. Internal descriptions alone do not prove that no substrate exists, or that every consistent structure contains genuine physical observers.

### Possibility as a serious organizing concept

Deutsch’s constructor theory gives possibility an unusually explicit role. It seeks to formulate physical laws through which transformations are possible and impossible, rather than only through evolution from initial conditions. Its possibilities are constrained physical tasks, not everything a person can imagine. This provides a precedent for changing the explanatory vocabulary while retaining exact restrictions and consequences.[^poss-constructor]

For your book, the useful question is whether *relations of permission and prohibition* could be more basic than a narrated sequence of material events. “Everything is possible” supplies less explanatory structure than specifying which relations can coexist and why.

### Cheap descriptions and rich worlds

Algorithmic probability offers one mathematical interpretation of economy. A schematic simplicity prior assigns greater weight to shorter descriptions, often expressed through a factor such as $2^{-K}$, where $K$ is description length in a specified computational framework. Schmidhuber’s speed prior also penalizes computational cost. These are defined weighting choices, not consequences of possibility alone.[^poss-speed]

The important distinction is between an economical *rule* and the detailed state it generates or describes. A short probabilistic law can allow overwhelmingly many complicated outcomes. A highly regular outcome can have a short description while being extraordinarily unlikely under that law.

Consider 100 independent fair coin flips. “All heads” is easy to describe. It is only one of $2^{100}$ equally likely strings. The category “roughly half heads” contains enormously many strings, most individually awkward to describe. The probability calculation and the economy of a particular string answer different questions. This elementary counterexample does not refute a cosmic simplicity prior; it shows why that prior must be specified rather than borrowed from ordinary counting.

### Probability and information geometry before Hilbert space

Caticha’s entropic dynamics reconstructs nonrelativistic quantum behavior using probabilistic inference, constraints and geometric structure; Hilbert-space machinery enters late as a convenient representation. It assumes definite particle positions and substantial additional structure. Crucially, its probability and phase fields guide inference: the paper explicitly does **not** claim that probabilities exert forces on particles.[^poss-caticha]

This is a valuable inversion for your project. It shows a worked route in which familiar quantum formalism is not the first premise. It also isolates the point at which your interpretation would add something: turning inferential preference into an ontological account of why a physical configuration changes.

A 2025 paper by Caticha and Saleem develops relational entropic-dynamics models using best matching between configurations. The models retain absolute simultaneity and Euclidean spatial geometry. The authors distinguish this programme from Rovelli’s relational quantum mechanics. It is a concrete recent lead, not a completed removal of space and time.[^poss-bestmatching]

### Geometry from quantum relations

Van Raamsdonk studies holographic examples in which changing entanglement changes the connectivity and separation of the dual geometry. Ryu and Takayanagi relate boundary entanglement entropy to an extremal-geometric quantity (a minimal area in their static construction). These are technically substantial reasons to investigate geometry as dependent on quantum organization.[^poss-vr][^poss-rt]

ER=EPR proposes a connection between entanglement and Einstein–Rosen bridges, starting from entangled black holes and conjecturing a wider correspondence. It is not a general law equating any pair’s entanglement strength with their ordinary spatial distance.[^poss-er]

Swingle offers a particularly apt condensed-matter connection: organizing quantum entanglement by scale can define an additional geometric structure. This helps sharpen your intuition that relationships may precede coordinates. The quantum state, its organization and the construction rules already do considerable work.[^poss-swingle]

Rovelli’s RQM makes a different move: physical states or values are relative to other physical systems, without privileging human observers. That supports your separation of relational registration from conscious experience. It does not itself derive spatial distance from entanglement.[^poss-rqm]

An editorial formulation worth keeping is: **our familiar spatial description might summarize an organization of relations whose fundamental description is not spatial.** Calling those underlying relations “possibilities” remains a proposal to develop; entanglement supplies specific mathematical content that the broader word presently lacks.

### Emergence and symmetry breaking without arranging every atom

Wilson’s renormalization-group treatment explains how critical scaling can follow from flow toward a fixed point, with some microscopic differences becoming irrelevant at larger scales. This supports a disciplined version of your self-organization intuition: robust large-scale behavior need not preserve or require specification of every microscopic detail.[^poss-wilson]

Symmetry breaking supplies another useful distinction. A rule can respect a symmetry while a particular organized state does not. A magnet’s chosen orientation illustrates how an outcome can possess less symmetry than the governing description. It does not mean all symmetries are equally likely, or that unrestricted possibilities automatically possess the invariances needed by physics.

Your proposed “symmetric possibilities, differentiated reality” passage should therefore distinguish the **symmetry of the allowed description**, the **symmetry of a statistical distribution**, and the **symmetry of one configuration**. They can differ. Nor does adding broken symmetry always increase complexity: a uniformly ordered state may be simpler to describe than a disordered one. The appropriate measure of complexity has to be named.

I would move the analogy away from a factory assembling each Planck cell. A better research question is: **could a broad family of underlying relational arrangements yield the same effective physics, making that physics insensitive to many microscopic differences?** This extends the lesson of universality without treating it as a derivation of the cosmos.

## Energy and time give us a precise mathematical foothold

Energy is already a property or quantity in physical theory, rather than an independently poured substance. In a familiar equilibrium setting,

\[
p_i=Z^{-1}e^{-\beta E_i},\qquad
E_i-E_j=-\beta^{-1}\ln(p_i/p_j),
\quad \beta=(k_BT)^{-1}.
\]

The algebra connects **relative energy** to **relative probability**, once the equilibrium model and temperature are specified. It does not turn arbitrary probabilities into joules. If $p_i$ groups many microstates, degeneracy contributes; its logarithm generally describes an effective free-energy landscape rather than bare microscopic energy.

The corresponding quantum equilibrium relation is

\[
\rho=Z^{-1}e^{-\beta H},\qquad
-\ln\rho=\beta H+\ln Z\,I.
\]

For a full-rank finite-dimensional density operator, $-\ln\rho$ defines a modular Hamiltonian. Connes and Rovelli’s thermal-time hypothesis explores how a state together with an observable algebra can supply a time flow; in equilibrium it reproduces the usual Hamiltonian flow up to scale. This is a real mathematical inversion relevant to your question.[^poss-thermal]

It leaves important choices visible: which state, which observables, which scale, and why that flow should be physically meaningful. An equilibrium flow alone does not explain the direction of entropy increase. These are productive questions for your *relative conformational bias*, without prematurely identifying it with temperature, energy or modular flow.

Noether provides a constraint on a successful continuation: under the theorem’s conditions, continuous symmetries of the action yield conservation laws, with time-translation symmetry giving energy conservation in the appropriate setting. The theorem does not generate energy from possibility, and its general-covariance complications matter. Your inversion should eventually recover this relationship, not obtain an origin claim by changing its wording.[^poss-noether]

Two other approaches extend the time question. Page–Wootters models describe evolution through correlations between a subsystem and a physical clock within a globally constrained state. Recent work examines how the account changes with nonideal clocks. Barbour, Koslowski and Mercati find a gravitational arrow associated with growing complexity and records in a restricted Newtonian model.[^poss-clocks][^poss-arrow]

These routes suggest a more precise question than “what makes the next moment happen?”: **what structure allows an internal system to serve as a clock, carry asymmetric records, and identify dependable change relative to other systems?** A possible answer must distinguish temporal ordering, duration, causal propagation and the thermodynamic arrow; they are related problems, not synonyms.

Jacobson’s derivation of Einstein’s equation as an equation of state is another useful inversion. Horizon entropy and the heat–temperature relation impose gravitational dynamics, given substantial geometric and thermodynamic premises. It suggests that familiar gravitational equations could encode deeper collective organization.[^poss-jacobson]

Gravity cannot generally be treated as a brake on change: it also drives collapse and structure formation. Nor does a finite speed of light establish universal friction. Lieb–Robinson bounds provide a better controlled analogy: suitable local quantum interactions yield an effective bound on information propagation. Those models already assume a notion of locality and time; they do not derive relativistic spacetime from nothing.[^poss-lr]

## What still needs to be supplied

### A weighting rule

The most important missing bridge is from **permitted** to **favored**. A very large or infinite collection does not come with a unique probability measure. A relabeling, choice of coordinates, or different way to count equivalent descriptions can change naïve claims about what is common. Even once a prior is selected, a high probability does not become logical certainty.

Anthropic reasoning restricts observations to circumstances compatible with the observer doing the observing. It does not independently establish that our universe has the minimum complexity required for us, that its parameters must be as they are, or that we are a randomly selected typical observer. Hartle and Srednicki explicitly examine that last additional assumption.[^poss-typical]

An instructive test is whether the proposed weighting favors sustained worlds with reliable records over isolated observer-like configurations with misleading apparent histories. Cosmological Boltzmann-brain problems show why “there can be an observer here” is insufficient. The appropriate comparison depends on the model; neither kind of observer is automatically favored by every simplicity measure.[^poss-bb]

### Dynamics rather than a probability slogan

Even a known equilibrium distribution generally does not uniquely determine a dynamics. Many transition processes can have the same stationary distribution while differing in rates and trajectories. Probabilities alone therefore do not fix a force, clock or causal structure.

The useful version of “a particle feels the more possible direction” is a question about whether constrained relational statistics can produce an effective drift or interaction. “Feels” is a metaphor for physical response, with no consciousness assigned to a particle. “More possible” needs to become a specified probability, amplitude, multiplicity or other mathematical quantity. Quantum interference is an immediate reason not to collapse these into one scalar preference.

There is also a viable alternative to an external search that tries configurations in sequence: specify mutually compatible relations over an entire history, then investigate whether internal clocks and causal descriptions can be recovered. This removes the premature external clock. It does not yet explain why one weighting of histories should apply.

### Conditions on the actual cosmos

The hot Big Bang, proposed inflationary stages, and an absolute origin of spacetime are different claims. Inflation can address features of early-universe evolution within a physical model; it does not by itself explain why that model or its initial conditions exist. Planck observations constrain inflationary possibilities and spatial curvature, rather than certify a perfectly spherical universe or a unique creation process.[^poss-planck]

There is no established Planck-sized grid of space or clock tick of time. A minimal measurable length, discrete geometry and a regular lattice are distinct proposals. Their differences matter even within quantum-gravity research.[^poss-length] The identity $t_P=\ell_P/c$ uses $c$ in defining the Planck scales; it cannot independently explain why a causal speed exists.

Likewise, mass is not all awaiting Higgs symmetry breaking. Most ordinary nucleon mass comes from QCD dynamics rather than simply adding quark rest masses. This actually helps your broader interest in relational contributions, while placing a limit on the particular Higgs account.[^poss-mass]

At the most ambitious level, “all possibilities exist” can become so permissive that every conceivable observation fits. To gain explanatory power, the proposal must distinguish expectation from mere accommodation. Recovering familiar results from independent assumptions, or finding a consequence that could go wrong, would accomplish more than making an existing result describable afterward.

## Nagel and the limits of an internal view

Nagel is useful here as a discipline of perspective. In *The View from Nowhere*, the attempt to understand ourselves within the world also exposes the contribution of our own constitution to that understanding. His discussion does not reduce objectivity to a fraud or establish an impassable boundary to knowledge.[^poss-nagel]

For the book, separate an internal observer’s evidence, a mathematical description that includes the observer, and a claim to know the ultimate ontology. The second is possible without possessing the third. A mathematical model can include its observers without granting its author an actual exit from the universe.

Being internal does not itself prove that a substrate is inaccessible. The stronger case is **underdetermination**: if distinct proposed foundations yield all the same accessible consequences, those consequences cannot choose between them. If they yield different consequences, indirect tests may help. Neither outcome is settled simply by our location inside reality.

Your fish analogy makes a legitimate possibility vivid: some concepts may exceed a particular cognitive architecture. It does not identify which human questions are permanently unreachable. A mathematical obstruction also needs its own proof; Gödel is not a universal certificate of cosmic unknowability.

The “2 + 2 = 1” example belongs to the modeling boundary. Four separate drops can merge into one drop. The rule for individuation changed; ordinary addition did not fail. This is a powerful point about objecthood: successful arithmetic in an application depends on what is being counted and whether those units persist under the operation. It supports examining hidden modeling assumptions without suggesting that contradiction supplies an origin mechanism.

The familiar “same thinking” quotation should not be attributed to Einstein here without a secure primary source for that wording. Your argument does not need the borrowed endorsement. Nor should the book claim physicists universally assume fundamental spacetime: several of the research programmes above explicitly investigate alternatives.

## A useful next formulation and its place in the book

The research question can now be made fairly compact:

> Can a structure of mutually constraining possibilities support stable internal records, a quantum account of alternatives, and effective spacetime, such that energy and dynamics arise as features of those relations? If economical structures are privileged, what defines that privilege without assuming the physical world the account aims to explain?

A manageable conceptual programme would begin with a small class of relational structures rather than an unformalized totality of everything. Identify equivalent descriptions so that relabeling does not manufacture extra worlds. State which operations compose structures and which constraints exclude combinations. Then investigate a particular probability or amplitude assignment, distinguishing its independent assumptions from anything derived.

The first worthwhile result need not be a theory of everything. It might be a demonstration that a defined class produces persistent records, or that a specified composition principle requires quantum rather than classical probability, or that an effective geometry is robust to microscopic changes. Conversely, a failure can locate the ingredient missing from the conjecture. Simply choosing a model already containing quantum mechanics and renaming its coordinates “possibilities” would teach us much less.

For the book, I recommend this division:

| Location | Work it should do |
| --- | --- |
| Chapter Φ | Establish the distinction between conceivability, consistency, modeling and warrant. Plant the question of reality without answering it by definition. |
| Interlude Φ | Explain state spaces, probability and the additional structure of quantum Hilbert space. State plainly that this is not yet the proposed prephysical possibility. |
| Chapter 1 | Present the relational-actuality conjecture in the author’s voice, then let the mother’s measurements, symmetry and the physics test and complicate it. Preserve the emotional story. |
| Interlude 1 | Use the implemented silicon world as a controlled analogy for internal description versus substrate. Keep the external engineer in the analogy from becoming a cosmic conclusion. |
| Interlude 12 | Develop clocks, entropy, records, propagation and the unresolved difference between ordering and the felt passage of time. |
| Chapter 13 and Interlude 14 | Return to particle identity and then the deeper quantum possibilities where their technical treatment belongs. |

One possible Φ bridge, for later author adoption:

> We can specify a space of possible states without claiming that any of them has happened. Quantum theory gives such a space a particular structure: amplitudes, interference and the relations between joint and separate systems. Whether that structure describes reality or participates in constituting it is a further question. I will eventually ask it from inside the world doing the describing (the only address I have).

The major conceptual gain is that **reality as internally effective relational structure** can be explored without committing in advance to a Planck lattice, cosmic computation, a preferred interpretation of quantum mechanics or a universal force of simplicity. Those are candidate continuations to compare. Their eventual relationship has to be earned.
