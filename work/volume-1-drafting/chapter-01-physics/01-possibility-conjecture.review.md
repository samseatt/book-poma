# Chapter 1 — Possibility before physics: proposed statement

**Separate review draft · 4 October 2026 · not adopted into the manuscript.** Authorial conjecture, not an established physical theory. The source notes identify conceptual precedents and the observer-selection qualification; they do not validate the proposed ontology. The silicon/chip analogy is reserved for Interlude 1.

## Proposed passage

Before I give the universe any matter, I want to see how much work possibility can do. What follows is a logical conjecture I want to carry into physics and expose to its most demanding findings. I am putting it on the chopping board, with no exemption for the hand that put it there.

By possibility, I mean a way that distinctions and relationships could be arranged without contradiction. I have supplied no space for them to occupy, no clock for them to obey, no primordial energy awaiting instructions. Admittedly, I have already helped myself to distinctions, relationships and consistency. Even this free lunch arrives with a bill. The question is how much of reality those few assumptions might buy.

A possibility considered alone tells us little. Place it in relation to another and compatibility becomes a question: what can hold together, what excludes what, what follows if certain relations hold? This is the first sense in which I imagine possibilities “interacting.” Physical interaction would have to emerge further along. Even “emerge” needs care when time is among the things we hope to explain.

Here is my wager: a sufficiently structured web of such relationships might constitute a reality for its own inhabitants. What we call a thing would be an identifiable configuration within that web, its properties arising through its relations. Energy, matter and spacetime would have to become intelligible within the resulting account. They would have no privileged place in the opening inventory. I wonder whether anything needs to exist beyond this relational structure for existence to acquire all the solidity we demand of it.[^v1-01c-conjecture-01]

Within such a structure, some configurations might constrain others in repeatable ways. Relationships could carry records; records could make comparison possible. Where those constraints support dependable inference, an inhabitant would encounter order. I am proposing that this order is part of what gives reality its apparent independence and persistence. It need not make every outcome predictable. A world with reliable probabilities could still keep its inhabitants thoroughly surprised.

I call the proposed preference among configurations *relative conformational bias*. That phrase names the unfinished part of the argument. Why should any configuration be favoured? I suspect that economy of structure matters: a small set of relations capable of supporting many consequences. But if I merely announce that simplicity wins, I have smuggled a law into the premises and congratulated myself on discovering it in the conclusion. Occam can help us choose an explanation. Making economy explain existence is a further task.

Our own position adds a peculiar difficulty. We would be configurations within whatever we are trying to understand. There was no separately existing me waiting to be assigned a fortunate universe. The conditions that permit this inquiry also permit its inquirer. That is the useful force of anthropic reasoning here. It constrains what we could observe; it does not tell us how many possible worlds there are, how they should be weighted, or why this particular order holds.[^v1-01c-conjecture-02]

Nor does our internal position prove that deeper structure must remain unknowable. We routinely infer things we cannot inspect directly. But if different foundations produce exactly the same accessible consequences, our evidence may never distinguish them. Reality could be relational structure all the way down, or rest on something whose workings appear to us as physical law. We should be careful about declaring either the last turtle.

So the challenge is to discover whether this picture can explain anything without quietly borrowing the thing it promises to explain. Can relationships account for persistent objects? Can an ordering internal to them account for time? Can we recover the specific regularities that measurement reveals, and identify some conceivable finding that would force us to abandon the picture?

Let us take it to the evidence. That is where a creation story should risk losing its job.

## Editorial boundaries and Interlude 1 reservation

The proposed passage introduces a research question, not a derivation from nothing. It interprets possibility-interaction initially as compatibility and constraint so that motion, forces and external time are not silently assumed. Relational existence remains a conjecture, with a distinction between regularity and complete prediction. Logical compatibility alone has not been shown to entail an experienced world. The passage deliberately leaves that burden visible.

Preserve the author's silicon/computer analogy for development in Interlude 1: an internal investigator could infer some architecture without uniquely identifying its ultimate implementation. Present it as a stipulated thought experiment; an infinite random substrate alone does not guarantee a functioning computer. A future technical treatment needs allowed configurations, a probability measure or generation process, working dynamics and the necessary physical operating conditions. Do not adopt the illustrative one-in-10^1000 number as an estimate. Nor does being internal automatically forbid inference about a substrate. No Interlude 1 text was drafted or changed in this task.
