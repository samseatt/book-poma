## Still open — author work, not failed adoption

**Cosmic reprise.** Its cheap-inflation argument requires a measure or weighting over possibilities and appropriate initial conditions. Inflation is not established as the cheapest route to a universe, nor a general solution to Boltzmann brains. The monkey's repeated lucky leap is an analogy, not a physical model of those observers. The paragraph remains verbatim because the author explicitly reserved it for manual work. “If Alice was right” also treats Alice as a character, contrary to the newer section-device convention; consider changing that during the same pass. Do not describe this adoption as certifying that paragraph's scientific argument.

**Informational gravity.** The remaining scientific challenge is to identify the shared measurable quantity, conditions and prediction that would distinguish this proposal from a family of familiar mechanisms. Long persistence alone does not entail replication or growth. The revised prose makes this an open research step while retaining the name and direction.

A potentially useful research pair for that work is Jeremy England, [“Statistical physics of self-replication”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1179), *J. Chem. Phys.* 139, 121923 (2013), and Artemy Kolchinsky, [“Thermodynamic dissipation does not bound replicator growth and decay rates”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01130) (2024), DOI 10.1063/5.0213466. Their abstracts were read in this pass, not both full papers. The later critique distinguishes decay into waste from time-reversed replication. This is a promising contested lead, not evidence that the author's law has been derived; neither paper was inserted as such a proof.
