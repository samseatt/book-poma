## Interlude 9

**Machine interfaces.** Hyrum's own formulation and semver.org establish the contract/versioning concepts. RFC 9413 critiques unconditional tolerance; RFC 8446 §§4.1.2/4.2.1 documents TLS legacy-version signaling. RFCs 9000 and 8701 establish QUIC and GREASE; the latter deliberately advertises reserved unsupported values, not arbitrary corrupt handshakes. CLIP's original paper specifies joint normalized image/text embeddings and roughly 400 million image–text pairs. Anthropic's November 2024 MCP and Google's April 2025 A2A announcements are dated interoperability examples, not guarantees of common semantics or safe action.

**Human interfaces.** Apple's 23 October 2001 announcement confirms exactly six weeks after September 11; the first device had a scroll wheel, not the later click wheel. Norman's own affordance/signifier explanation supports the local correction. Johnson and Goldstein's full original paper distinguishes country effective-consent figures (4.25–27.5% versus 85.9–99.98%) from the randomized default experiment; these are not transplant rates or identical populations. DSA Article 25 addresses manipulative online interfaces, not a universal legal definition of fraud. The AI Act's workplace/education emotion-inference restrictions have scope and exceptions. Product and regulation claims are dated, not promises that rules or device behavior remain unchanged forever.

**Spatial, voice and neural interfaces.** Varjo describes the engineering latency target; Hoffman et al.'s experiment supports vergence–accommodation fatigue. Apple's guide supports People Awareness behavior. Nielsen's own essay supports his stated interface-paradigm interpretation. Stivers et al. establish the cross-language turn-taking result. OpenAI's launch report supplies its reported 232 ms minimum / 320 ms average audio latency, not an independent benchmark for every voice system. Willett and Metzger's original 2023 articles support participant/task-specific decoding demonstrations, not universal thought reading. Neuralink and Be My Eyes reports are identified as company/service reports. Barrett et al.'s review supports limits on inferring emotion from facial movements. Some other historical product examples retain their original notes and a preprint/product-refresh reminder; they are not represented as exhaustive market research.

**Learning and feedback.** Netflix's own 2012 technical retrospective distinguishes deployed factorization/RBM components from the final ensemble whose incremental accuracy did not justify deployment costs. Google's original YouTube paper distinguishes candidate retrieval from ranking. Li et al. support contextual-bandit exploration; Chaney et al. support investigating feedback/confounding through simulation. Neither proves the new toy model's assumptions about real users. The replacement model, parameters and outputs are fully recorded in `REPRODUCE_FEED.md`, code, JSON and CSV. Its exact percentages are now reproducible. The input SVG was regenerated from these outputs while retaining its design.

**Governors and interruption.** Microsoft's UAC design guidance, Vance et al.'s longitudinal warning-habituation study, Meta boundary guidance and Apple's People Awareness guide support the examples. NHTSA's dated 2024 final rule set the principal 2029 compliance date; the prose no longer guarantees future implementation. Cicchino's AEB crash result remains an observational estimate for a particular crash category. Eriksson/Stanton's takeover study concerns noncritical simulator transitions, not every emergency. Predictive processing supplies an analogy, not a settled law of awareness. Local coupling, surprise-times-stakes thresholds and self-forming governors remain the author's research proposal. Rights and permissions override the score; silence alone cannot establish safety or trust.
