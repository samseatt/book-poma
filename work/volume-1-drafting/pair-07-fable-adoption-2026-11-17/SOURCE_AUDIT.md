### v1-i7-pagerank

Indexed primary/author text or institutional account inspected. 1999 report, convergence discussion and figure 4

Sergey Brin and Lawrence Page, "The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine" (1998), *Computer Networks and ISDN Systems* 30: 107–117, §2.1, which describes the "random surfer" and a damping factor usually set to 0.85; Lawrence Page, Sergey Brin, Rajeev Motwani and Terry Winograd, "The PageRank Citation Ranking: Bringing Order to the Web," Stanford InfoLab technical report (1999), which reports convergence in roughly fifty iterations on a graph of about 322 million links. Google was incorporated in September 1998. The 1998 paper writes the jump term as $(1-d)$; dividing by $N$, as here, makes the ranks a probability distribution summing to one. Pages without outbound links are treated as jumping anywhere. With the jump included, the surfer's walk is a Markov chain with a unique stationary distribution, computable by power iteration. The figure's percentages are computed from the formula for its six-page graph. Search ranking has long combined PageRank with many other signals. [1998 paper](https://research.google/pubs/the-anatomy-of-a-large-scale-hypertextual-web-search-engine/). For the figure, A links to B; B to A; C to A and F; D to A; E to A and F; F to E. At damping 0.85 their stationary shares are 41.408, 37.697, 2.5, 2.5, 8.655 and 7.241 percent. Circle sizes are illustrative; percentages carry the numerical result. [1999 report, convergence discussion and figure 4](https://storm.cis.fordham.edu/~gweiss/selected-papers/classic-pagerank-paper.pdf). The reported 52 iterations are an experiment, not a general bound on power-iteration convergence.
