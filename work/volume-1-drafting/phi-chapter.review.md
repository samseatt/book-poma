CHAPTER Φ

# The Archer’s Compass

*The Unprovable Beginning*

*ORIENTATION*

> “At the foundation of well-founded belief lies belief that is not founded.” — Ludwig Wittgenstein, *On Certainty*[^v1-phi-c-certainty]

The basket was still there. I had needed to see it.

Those two statements describe different facts. The first concerns a woven object somewhere toward the front of an aircraft. The second concerns a small boy whose world had recently become unreliable. His parents had returned; his parents, as he understood the term, had disappeared. The adults could explain the arrangement. He could not live inside their explanation yet.

I was that boy. I am also the man arranging his distress into paragraphs, which introduces another interested party.

Between us lie decades of remembering, forgetting, being told things, and learning words with which to make the whole affair sound inevitable. It was probably a Fokker. Perhaps I touched the basket; perhaps seeing it was enough. I have left those uncertainties where they belong. An exact aircraft model would improve the paperwork. It would not improve the memory.

What survives is the crossing: the unfamiliar people who were my real parents, the familiar life receding, and the basket that could still be found. I call this my experiential Big Bang because it is where the story available to me begins. I cannot certify that consciousness switched on there. I had neglected to bring a neuroscientist.

The world, meanwhile, had been managing without my account of it. The aircraft did not acquire lift when I became persuaded of aviation. My fear was real, but its explanation of the adults was wrong. The basket’s existence did not depend on its visibility, although my peace of mind very much did.

Here, before physics or politics, we already have most of our difficulties. Something happens. Something encounters it. A version becomes available to that encounter. Later, another version becomes a story. At each crossing, something is preserved and something changes.

Before I send an arrow through any grand domain, I want to inspect the instrument with which I decide where it went.

## Experience, Memory, Thought

We use *experience* generously. A person experiences grief; a bridge experiences a load; a company experiences difficulties that everyone except its management calls bankruptcy. The grammar accommodates them all. It does not establish that they have the same inner life.

Here I begin with the human meaning: the world as it becomes present to someone. Sensation contributes, but so do attention, remembered patterns, expectation, the body’s condition, and the question one is already trying to answer. There is an encounter and there is the encounter as lived. Their relationship is intimate enough to tempt us into treating them as identical.

My working picture is of a brain continually interpreting what reaches it against what it has learned. That is a useful orientation, not a finished theory of consciousness. The account of how physical processes become felt experience remains contested; supplying the word *processing* does not settle it.[^v1-phi-c-consciousness]

Still, one inversion is available before we know the machinery. We ordinarily describe experience as the world entering us. Consider how much of us is already waiting at the entrance. In the aircraft, the same people could be parents in one account and alarming strangers in another. The difference mattered without requiring two aircraft or two sets of adults.

This does not make reality a private hallucination. It makes our access to reality an achievement with moving parts. If I misread a step and fall, both the interpretation and the floor contribute to the result. Only one can be corrected by further reflection while I remain seated.

Most encounters leave no recollection I can call back. Those that do are not necessarily stored as miniature films. Remembering is constructive: elements can be selected, recombined and influenced by what we later know. The same flexibility that helps us use the past can also distort it.[^v1-phi-c-memory]

A memory is a compression algorithm with opinions.

I keep that description because it catches something important, provided we do not mistake it for an anatomical diagram. The compression has a purpose; the opinions have a history. A childhood terror can remain vivid while the colour of a seat disappears. Later understanding may supply a sensible explanation that was wholly unavailable to the frightened child. The explanation belongs in the account, but it should carry its own date.

There is an ethical consequence hiding inside this modest distinction. When I tell a memory, I possess neither an unedited recording nor a licence to manufacture one. Uncertainty is part of the material. If I give my younger self an insight I acquired forty years later, I have made him a better philosopher and myself a less reliable witness.

Thought makes the arrangement stranger. It can bring what is absent into the present, combine things that never met, and rehearse consequences before submitting a body to them. Memory helps furnish that workshop; it does not exhaust what thinking does. A remembered basket, an imagined basket and the concept *basket* are available to thought in different ways. None can carry the groceries.

Yet the distinction between imagination and knowledge is not that one uses representations and the other somehow escapes them. A good explanation is also a construction. The question is what disciplines its construction: which observations constrain it, which alternatives it must survive, and what would make us change it.

We will need this distinction repeatedly. An imagined future may be useful without being a prediction. A model may explain something without resembling it. A memory may be honest without being complete. Confusing those categories gives us some excellent speeches and several avoidable disasters.

I also suspect that the ancestry of encounter is much older than a brain. A physical interaction can leave one thing bearing a trace of another; in quantum measurement, relationships among a system, an apparatus and its surroundings become central to what can be recorded. I am tempted to see our elaborate human knowing as growing somewhere along that very long history of the world becoming consequential to itself. That is a philosophical thread I want to follow. It is not evidence that a detector feels anything, or that consciousness summons matter into existence.[^v1-phi-c-registration]

Physics will have a say. Biology and neuroscience will complicate the inheritance. For now, the useful move is to keep the question open without allowing the word *experience* to answer it for us.

## Language, Story, and the Human OS

A private encounter becomes a public problem when I try to tell you about it.

I can say *basket*, and you can supply an object from your own inventory. It need not look quite like mine for us to proceed. I can say *home*, and the differences become more consequential. I can say *freedom*, and we may spend a century discovering that we packed different luggage.

Language makes this exchange possible through shared practices, distinctions and expectations. It also lets a disagreement travel undetected inside a familiar word. A conversation can appear to advance while its participants are departing in different directions with admirable fluency.

Wittgenstein’s later work is useful here because it asks us to attend to what words do in the activities of a life. An instruction, a description, a joke and a promise do not acquire their force in quite the same way. His discussion of meaning as use explicitly concerns a large class of cases, not an all-purpose formula that dissolves every question.[^v1-phi-c-language]

I find the restraint as useful as the insight. Philosophical slogans often arrive with their exceptions removed, presumably to reduce shipping costs.

Take *mine*. Attached to the basket, it might express a child’s familiarity and attachment. On a customs form it might concern ownership. In a dispute it might invoke a rule, a purchase, an inheritance, or the fact that I am currently the largest person in the room. One syllable can summon affection, law or a small war. Before resolving the dispute, we may need to discover which kind we are having.

This is one job philosophy does well: noticing when an answer has been made to solve the wrong question. What exists? How do I know? What does this word permit me to claim? What matters enough to act upon? Those questions overlap, but they are not interchangeable. A measurement does not settle an obligation simply by arriving with decimals. Nor does a sincere moral conviction tell us whether a physical mechanism works.

On top of language we build stories. A story places events in an order, assigns significance, suggests a cause, and gives the listener somewhere to stand. Without some such selection, my life would be an unsearchable accumulation of afternoons. With too much selection, it becomes a campaign biography.

The danger is not that a story has form. It is that its form can quietly take over the work of evidence. *This happened after that* becomes *this happened because of that*. An ending makes the earlier events seem as though they had been rehearsing for it. The person telling the story becomes the person who understood it all along.

I will try to resist that last promotion. The man writing this book has enough advantages over the child without also stealing his confusion.

Some stories do more than describe a world. They help organize one. A currency, an office or a property title depends on collectively maintained practices and recognized powers in ways that a stone’s mass does not. Searle calls attention to this distinction through institutional facts and status functions.[^v1-phi-c-institutions] A border can be made by people and still stop a person with entirely real force. *Constructed* is a poor synonym for *imaginary* when there are guards.

This is what I mean when I call story a human operating system. The phrase is a metaphor for coordination: shared accounts help tell us what counts, who may do what, and why a particular arrangement deserves obedience. The metaphor does not mean that a society is merely software, or that its food and fuel arrive by narrative consent.

It does invite a reversal. We tell stories to make collective life possible. Once established, those stories can begin assigning the terms on which we are allowed to live. An instrument for cooperation becomes an authority over its makers. Eventually someone asks why the arrangement exists, and receives the arrangement’s own brochure.

There will be plenty of time for those castles. Here we need only notice the transaction. A word can describe a constraint or impose one. The same sentence may do both. If we cannot tell which work it is performing, the mirror has become an instruction manual while we were admiring the view.

## Knowing What We Think We Know

I could be completely certain of the wrong adults. Certainty, then, deserves a smaller office than we usually give it.

It is a condition of the believer. Truth concerns what is the case. Justification concerns the reasons available for a belief. These can come apart. A confident person may be wrong; a hesitant one may have excellent evidence; a lucky guess may land on the truth without having travelled by a dependable route.

The distinction matters even when we get the answer right. Imagine looking at an apparently working clock which, unknown to you, stopped exactly twelve hours ago. It happens to display the correct time. You believe it, have what ordinarily seems a reasonable basis, and are right. Something is still missing from the achievement we normally mean by *knowing*. A coincidence has impersonated a connection.

Cases of this family trouble the simple equation of knowledge with justified true belief. Gettier’s compact 1963 paper made the problem difficult to ignore.[^v1-phi-c-gettier] We need not solve the ensuing literature to inherit the useful suspicion: a conclusion should be connected to its truth in a way that is more robust than good luck.

That suspicion reaches well beyond philosophy departments. If a forecasting system predicts a crisis by noticing a feature that happened to accompany earlier crises, a successful prediction does not yet tell us whether the feature will remain useful. If my explanation can accommodate every possible outcome, its survival tells us little. I may have built a theory with no exits for contradictory evidence.

The trick is to ask what has actually been earned. Have we observed a regularity, identified a mechanism, excluded an alternative, or merely learned to predict within a particular range? Each can be valuable. Trouble begins when one is invoiced as another.

Hume pressed on a related difficulty: our expectation that familiar regularities will continue is not established by deductive logic. The fact that a sequence has held so far does not contain, as a logical consequence, the promise that it must hold tomorrow. Appealing to the past success of such expectations already uses the sort of inference we are trying to justify.[^v1-phi-c-hume]

This is an inconvenient discovery for creatures with appointments. We still have to expect the ground to support us and our words to retain enough meaning to order lunch. Inquiry proceeds through such reliance. The task is to make it examinable where examination matters, rather than pretend we rebuilt all knowledge from zero before breakfast.

Wittgenstein’s observation at the head of this chapter points toward that background. Doubt itself operates amid things we are not, at that moment, doubting. To test a scale, I need some confidence in the reference weight and in the practice of comparison. I cannot call every possible support into question simultaneously and still perform that particular test.[^v1-phi-c-certainty-background]

My reading of this is practical: begin where inquiry can get traction, then remain capable of revising what supports it. That is different from declaring the foundations sacred. A support can be necessary for this operation without being beyond all future examination.

Much of what any of us knows also arrives through other people. I have not personally repeated the experiments behind every instrument I use. Dependence is unavoidable; blind dependence is an additional choice. There is a difference between trusting an account because it makes its evidence and methods available, and trusting it because it has a large following or a solemn voice. Neither route eliminates judgment. One gives judgment more to work with.

Nor can all useful knowing be squeezed into statements ready for an examination. Knowing how to balance, knowing a person, and knowing a proposition call on different accomplishments. A catalogue of facts about affection will not make its compiler a good companion. There is room for tacit skill and lived understanding here, without granting either automatic authority on the composition of a vaccine or the solvency of a bank.

The compass I want is therefore less interested in announcing *I know* than in keeping track of the route: what I encountered, what I inferred, whose work I rely upon, and where the account might fail. Those distinctions are small enough to fit inside a sentence. They are large enough to change what a civilization does with one.

## What Follows, and What We Smuggle

Logic enters when we ask whether a conclusion actually follows from what we have allowed ourselves to assume.

That is a narrower task than deciding whether the premises describe the world, and much narrower than deciding whether the conclusion is desirable. It is also an extraordinary task. An argument can be assessed by someone who dislikes its author. In a species with our social habits, this is almost an act of rebellion.

Suppose all passengers are aboard, and the basket is a passenger. Then the basket is aboard. The inference has behaved itself; the classification has not. Now suppose everything aboard is safe, and the basket is aboard. The conclusion that the basket is safe follows, but only because we introduced an enormous promise in the first premise. The reassuring part of the argument was never supplied by logic. We packed it ourselves.

This is why a valid deduction can fail to describe reality. Validity concerns the relation between premises and conclusion: if the premises are true, that form of inference cannot deliver a false conclusion. A sound argument additionally has true premises. The extra word earns its salary.[^v1-phi-c-validity]

Everyday thinking mixes deduction with other moves. We extend a pattern from observed cases to unobserved ones. We propose an explanation for an otherwise puzzling event. We imagine an intervention and ask what might change. These moves do not become defective deductions simply because they offer no deductive guarantee. They do different jobs, under different liabilities.

The error is to change jobs halfway through without telling anyone. A correlation enters as an observation and leaves as a cause. A convention enters as an agreement and leaves as a law of nature. A value judgment arrives dressed as a calculation, its preferences already installed in what is being counted.

We should also watch the reverse transaction. An unwelcome finding can be demoted to “just a model” even when it survives demanding tests, while a comfortable intuition is promoted to “what everybody knows.” I would like to say I reserve these manoeuvres for my opponents. Unfortunately I have observed my own filing system.

Inversion is one way to disturb the arrangement. Instead of asking how people adapt to an institution, ask how the institution produces the behaviour it then claims to manage. Instead of taking a measure as a neutral report, ask whether people are now organizing their lives to satisfy it. The reversed question can reveal a relationship hidden by the original direction of explanation.

But reversal is not a certificate of insight. Sometimes the conventional direction is substantially right. Sometimes the arrow runs both ways. Sometimes a third process drives the pair. To invert a claim responsibly is to expose it to a second line of inquiry, not to award the opposite claim victory for being more entertaining.

The same restraint applies when we cross disciplines. A market and an ecosystem may share a useful structural feature without having the same mechanism. A neural network and a nervous system can illuminate each other while remaining very different things. The comparison earns its passage by preserving a relationship that matters. Once it begins transporting conclusions unsupported by that relationship, metaphor has started trafficking.

That is where philosophy and science can irritate each other productively. Philosophy asks what an explanation commits us to; science asks what the world permits us to retain. Neither benefits from an agreement never to enter the other’s room. Nor does either improve when its vocabulary is used as diplomatic immunity.

There is one more limit a compass cannot abolish. It can help establish a bearing; it cannot decide what is worth reaching. We may calculate how to increase a quantity with great precision and still have chosen the wrong quantity. A perfectly managed journey can end somewhere nobody should have been sent.

For the arrows of civilization, I will keep asking whether increased capacity also increases room for a worthwhile life, whose room expands, and whose is quietly requisitioned. Those are commitments open to argument, not discoveries concealed inside an equation. If I smuggle them into the mathematics, I deprive the reader of the chance to disagree with the actual choice.

## A Place for Nothing

The basket presents a small exercise in ontology. An empty basket, a missing basket and a basket whose contents I have not inspected are three different predicaments. Only one can be remedied by putting more toys in it.

*Nothing* does an unreasonable amount of work in ordinary speech. “There is nothing in the room” may mean no people, no furniture, nothing relevant to the search, or that the speaker has dismissed everything present as uninteresting. Even a rigorously evacuated chamber is not the philosopher’s absence of space, fields, laws and possibility. Physics gets to examine its vacuum without being made responsible for every meaning of the word *void*.

Mathematics performs a useful act of restraint. The empty set, ∅, is a specified mathematical object with no elements. Its emptiness is exact because the question has been made exact. That does not make it a demonstration that the universe arose from nothing. A definition is not a cosmological event.[^v1-phi-c-empty]

There is something almost comic in the power of the move. Once absence has a place in a system, we can reason about it rather than keep pointing at a missing thing. Zero can be a number. An empty collection can still be a collection. In a database, an unknown value can be represented without pretending to have measured zero. The distinctions look fussy until a missing reading is used to calculate a result.[^v1-phi-c-null]

This is the philosophical value of the empty place for me. It allows ignorance to remain legible. We can distinguish *no answer has been entered* from *the answer is none*. A culture that cannot tolerate an unfilled space will acquire answers rapidly. It may never recover from some of them.

But there is no need to make absence the secret substance of everything. The vacant place helps orient inquiry; it need not be granted a creation myth. Φ begins with a question about the ground of our claims. Chapter 1 will ask about the ground under our feet. The resemblance is useful precisely because the two are not yet the same question.

## The Castle That Ate Its Guarantee

By the beginning of the twentieth century, some of the most rigorous minds in Europe had reasons to be suspicious of their own foundations. This was not merely a fashionable loss of confidence. A contradiction had appeared close to the machinery by which mathematics tried to say what a collection was.

Russell’s paradox can be approached without ceremonial dress. Consider the collection of all sets that are not members of themselves. Is this collection a member of itself? If it is, it fails its own admission rule. If it is not, it qualifies. The difficulty exposes a problem in allowing unrestricted definitions to produce sets. The repair requires discipline about what may be constructed, not greater enthusiasm for the original rule.[^v1-phi-c-russell]

Frege’s logical foundations and Whitehead and Russell’s *Principia Mathematica* belonged to the effort to put mathematical reasoning on explicit footing. Hilbert pursued a distinct but related ambition: make the formal rules precise and establish the consistency of the mathematics built with them. His second problem in 1900 explicitly called for a proof that arithmetic’s axioms could not lead to contradiction.[^v1-phi-c-hilbert]

It is easy, after learning what happened next, to caricature this as a cathedral built by men who had never heard of weather. That misses the courage of the project. They were trying to expose assumptions to inspection. Mathematics would not have to rest on a reassuring tone of voice, or on the continued absence of an embarrassing incident. The foundation itself would be examined.

There were several ambitions in play. Consistency would keep contradiction out. Completeness would allow every statement in the relevant language to be settled. An effective method would make the rules something that could actually be followed. These properties are distinct; their attraction together is obvious. Imagine an intellectual institution that could resolve every case it admitted and establish that its procedure would never betray it.

In 1931, Gödel’s work showed why a crucial version of that promise could not be fulfilled. A consistent formal theory with effectively specified axioms and enough arithmetic cannot settle every arithmetical statement. Under the appropriate conditions, it also cannot prove its own consistency by its own means. These are results about precisely described theories. They do not announce that all reasoning is broken.[^v1-phi-c-godel]

The interlude will enter the mechanism. For this chapter, the startling fact is how the limit was found. Gödel did not discover a sloppy calculation in the building. He used rigorous reasoning to show a restriction on what such reasoning, organized in a particular formal way, could deliver. The instrument identified a limit in its own reach.

That is a more interesting upheaval than a bonfire of logic. The disappointment was produced by a success.

A truth unavailable to proof in one theory need not remain unavailable in a stronger one. Adding suitable principles can enlarge the reach; if the enlarged theory still meets the conditions, it has limits of its own. There is no promotion here to a final, effective theory of arithmetic that settles everything while remaining consistent.[^v1-phi-c-godel-extension]

I find this oddly hospitable. We are accustomed to imagining intellectual progress as the steady filling of a container. Here progress can also involve learning what the container is, which operations define its reach, and why changing it is a consequential act. The inquiry acquires another dimension. It has not become pointless because the old promise was too large.

There is a temptation to take the result on tour. An incomplete formal theory becomes an incomplete society; a constitution’s difficulties become Gödel in a wig. The analogy can provoke a worthwhile question, but the theorem has not proved that society’s arrangements are inevitable, nor that every legal dispute is undecidable. To make such a claim mathematical, we would have to specify the formal system and show that the result applies.

My more modest borrowing concerns intellectual conduct. Before demanding a guarantee, ask what kind of apparatus could supply it, on which assumptions, and from where. Before treating a boundary as failure, ask whether discovering it has made the instrument more trustworthy. And before praising human intuition for transcending machines, remember that being unable to formalize a conviction does not establish that it is true.

I would rather begin this book with an honest limit than smuggle in a guarantee and spend three volumes protecting it.

## On the Order of the Arrows

If the eleven archers that follow look suspiciously well-behaved, I should confess something: they did not begin as a clean diagram. They began as moves. Countries, homes, schools, occupations, kinds of certainty. Only later did I notice how a particular domain kept being dragged into the foreground, like a reluctant syllabus written in my passport stamps.

That is the human route through Volume I. Alongside it runs a technical one: chips, data, computing, software, networks, the systems through which our inventions have acquired a world of their own. Carbon moves through the chapters. Silicon answers in the interludes. They sometimes rhyme; neither has signed a contract to agree.

The order can also be read as a widening frame. We start with physical existence, then life, a brain, the person it helps constitute, and the social arrangements in which that person acts. Tools, economies, nations and beliefs enlarge the reach. Ecology brings the supporting world back into view; the informational sphere surrounds what now tries to understand and manage it. The sequence is an arrangement for inquiry, not a claim that reality was assembled according to my table of contents.

We will keep crossing the boundaries. A biological explanation may illuminate a cultural habit and then reach a point beyond which it explains very little. A machine may reveal a human capacity by reproducing it, or reveal our misunderstanding by succeeding differently. The useful connection is sometimes a resemblance; sometimes it is the precise place where the resemblance fails.

Our castles are the arrangements we inhabit. Their monsters are the troubles that their own strengths can help produce. The basket is what I want to remain capable of carrying forward when an arrangement has to change. That is enough equipment for now. The images can earn their subsequent appearances.

Two smaller companions will occasionally intrude. Magnesium becomes Mag, or Maggie; aluminium becomes Al, or Alexander. The pearl and the protector are associations I give the human names, not the etymologies of the elements. Magnesium’s name comes through Magnesia, aluminium’s through alum. Chemistry should not have to falsify its birth certificate to join a metaphor.[^v1-phi-c-companions]

They will appear where matter and meaning happen to brush against one another, sometimes to remind us of something, occasionally just to be irritating. A little irritation may be a reasonable admission fee for recurring characters made of elements.

Volume II will return to these domains with a diagnostic purpose: how their systems behave, where the apparent movement conceals stasis, and where an opening might exist. Volume III will take the openings seriously enough to design with them. We arrive there carrying the uncertainties as well as the findings. A design that forgets how its knowledge was acquired is liable to mistake inherited confidence for a tested foundation.

## A Bearing Worth Keeping

What would it mean for one of these arrows to advance?

Distance alone is an inadequate answer. We can become faster at performing an activity whose point we have ceased to examine. We can become exceptionally good at satisfying a measure that has parted company with the thing it was meant to represent. From inside the operation, everything looks like progress: more output, better throughput, shorter intervals between the same old disappointment.

The Still Arrow begins, for me, in that unease. Movement may preserve an arrangement as efficiently as it changes one. A civilization can expend immense intelligence improving its obedience to yesterday’s question.

This does not make every inheritance a prison or every novelty a release. Some constraints keep us alive. Some traditions retain knowledge their critics have not bothered to acquire. To decide whether an arrow has moved, we have to understand what was holding it, what the proposed motion changes, and what might be lost. Simply reversing a sign on the old direction can send us efficiently into another wall.

The habit I want to carry into the book is more demanding than either celebration or demolition. Let a claim encounter the best account of what resists it. Let a connection between disciplines expose a mechanism rather than merely furnish a handsome comparison. Let a conjecture develop enough shape that later evidence can damage it. A thought that survives only because it never risks being specific has arranged a very comfortable childhood for itself.

There will be places where the known facts permit several interpretations. There will also be places where one account has earned much more support than another. Respecting an edge does not require pretending the entire landscape is equally uncertain. We can stand firmly somewhere and still look beyond it.

I want science in this journey because it supplies unusually powerful ways of making an account answerable to something beyond our preference. I want philosophy because the questions, categories and purposes with which we approach the account are themselves open to examination. And I want the personal story because neither activity is being conducted by a creature who arrived without a history.

The child on the plane needed his basket. The adult can explain why its disappearance from view was not its disappearance from the world. Explanation has enlarged the account; it has not retroactively abolished the fear. That is a useful measure of what understanding can do, and of what it cannot simply replace.

Perhaps the most important thing to carry forward is this capacity to revise without erasing: to recover a distinction, change an explanation, preserve what mattered, and loosen what had begun to rule us. There is no theorem promising we will do it well. The absence of that promise leaves a great deal of work available.

For Φ, the first upheaval is already in hand. We sought a foundation that would settle everything of a certain kind, and found a rigorous reason why that foundation could not fulfil the promise. Yet the reasoning that revealed the limit remains usable. We have acquired both an instrument and a reason to handle it carefully.

The basket was there before I could account for it.

Now let us see what else was.
