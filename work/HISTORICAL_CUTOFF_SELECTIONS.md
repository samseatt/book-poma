# Volume III: proposed historical cutoff selections

Prepared 1 October 2026. **For author review; no historical passages have been inserted.** Current chapter numbering, 23–33, is used throughout.

The accepted chapter order remains workable. This selection develops the earlier historical review with stronger alternatives, an improved Making anchor, explicit US–Canada comparisons, and a proposed Indigenous thread. It supersedes earlier candidate preferences only as a new editorial proposal, not an author-approved lock.

**Event** means a dated occurrence; **condition** means a documented situation spanning the cutoff; **nearby** retains the event's actual date. Design connections below are editorial interpretations, not historical claims that the examples prove the proposed Neuverse arrangements. The Persian track covers Iranian history and its imperial worlds, including periods without a Persian national state. Seasonal symmetry remains optional.

## Persian / Iranian track — Empire of Ideology

| Chapter / cutoff | Preferred event or condition, and design connection | Leading alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **23 · Renewal — 750 BCE** | **Separate Median communities in western Iran, within an Assyrian imperial environment.** A condition, not a securely dated founding. Begin with the problem of forming common purpose among dispersed communities; do not put Deioces on a throne in 750. [Iranica: Media](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/media/) | **Assyrian expansion and tribute in 744 BCE.** A sharper nearby event about coordination under external pressure, but it moves the opening toward conquest rather than renewal. Same source. |
| **24 · Agency — 500 BCE** | **Darius's administration as seen in the Persepolis Fortification Archive, 509–493 BCE.** Rations and transfers place workers and travellers inside a functioning system. The design question is how being administered differs from having agency. [University of Chicago](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/Publications/ISACMP/isacmp3.pdf) | **The Ionian Revolt, beginning 499 BCE.** Stronger for resistance to imperial authority; less suited to the chapter's subtler problem of helpful systems making decisions for people. [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ionian-revolt/) |
| **25 · Economy — 250 BCE** | **The emergence of Arsacid power as Seleucid control fragments.** Use activity around 250 and the traditional era around 247, acknowledging disputed chronology. Ask what changes economically when the authority changes; do not invent a Parthian monetary liberation. Add the brief Alberta counterpoint proposed below. [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/arsacids-ii/?generate_pdf=1) | **Diodotus's separation of Bactria from Seleucid rule, conventionally around 250 BCE.** Another case of economic and political authority separating, but its chronology is also disputed and its geography is the wider Iranian world, not present-day Iran. [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/diodotus/) |
| **26 · Minds — 1 CE** | **Phraataces and Augustus's diplomatic dispute in 1 CE, leading to the meeting with Gaius Caesar in 2 CE.** Even royal titles were contested. Recognition, representation and negotiated meaning give a firmer bridge than the inherited Mithraic-network account. [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/phraates-v/) | **The joint coinage of Phraataces and Musa, attested from 1 CE.** A more visual opening about how public images and titles construct authority. Same source. Roman Mithraism should not be treated as a proven direct Persian export at this cutoff. |
| **27 · Commons — 251 CE** | **Religious plurality under Shapur I, including Mani's activity under royal protection.** A condition of the reign, not an edict issued in 251. The useful tension is access dependent on a ruler's permission: what makes shared cultural space secure? [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/shapur-i/) | **Shapur's Roman campaigns of approximately 252–256 and the associated forced resettlements.** More direct on dispossession, but chronologically less tidy and liable to repeat Making's treatment of coerced populations. [Iranica: deportations](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/deportations/deportations-ii-in-the-parthian-and-sasanian-periods/) |
| **28 · Making — 501 CE** | **Kavad's restored regime, followed by the war of 502 and capture of Amida in 503.** Sources describe deported craftspeople and connect resettlement to Arrajan's development as a linen centre. This is a stronger material story: productive capacity resides in people and skills, which power can seize. State the nearby dates and attribute the resettlement account; avoid precise captive totals. [Kavad](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kawad-i/kawad-i-reign/), [deportations](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/deportations/deportations-ii-in-the-parthian-and-sasanian-periods/), [Arrajan](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/arrajan-medieval-city-and-province-in-southwestern-iran-between-kuzestan-and-fars/) | **Kavad and the Mazdakite challenge to established privilege.** Preserve as an option if redistribution is emotionally important, but the programme and chronology are contested. It fits Economy more naturally than Making and should not become an exactly dated reform in 501. [Kavad study](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kawad-i/kawad-i-reign/) |
| **29 · Governance — 751 CE** | **Consolidation after the Abbasid revolution of 750, with Abu Muslim and Khurasan central to the new order.** The chapter begins after the overthrow: how does a successful coalition exercise and constrain the power it inherits? Do not call this a Persian national takeover. [Iranica: Abu Muslim](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abu-moslem-abd-al-rahman-b/) | **Talas, July 751.** An exact-year encounter involving Abbasid forces sent by Abu Muslim, Tang forces and their allies. Stronger on geopolitical boundaries, weaker on internal governance. Do not attach an unqualified “paper arrived because of this battle” story. [Iranica: Khurasan](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/khorasan-v-history-in-the-%CA%BFabbasid-period/) |
| **30 · Justice — 1001 CE** | **Ferdowsi continuing the Shahnameh.** The first version dates to 994; the major completion to 1010, with work documented around 1000–1002. Use the poem's examination of rulers and rightful conduct, not a fictitious completion in 1001. [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ferdowsi-i/) | **Mahmud of Ghazna's Indian campaign in 1001.** A more forceful contrast between successful conquest and just rule, but less rooted in the book's Persian literary and moral inheritance. [Iranica chronology](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chronology-of-iranian-history-part-1/) |
| **31 · Care — 1251 CE** | **Arghun Aqa's journey to the Mongol court in 1251–1252 and his account of Iran's disordered finances.** Census and tax changes followed in 1253. The question is whether making a population administratively legible relieves its suffering or merely improves extraction. This is an indirect Care connection, not an early welfare programme. [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/argun-aqa-a-mongol-administrator-in-iran-d-1275/) | **Sa'di's Bustan (1257) and Golestan (1258).** The strongest emotional alternative for care, suffering and ethical obligation. Its cost is explicit: six or seven years after the cutoff. I would consider it only as a deliberately later flowering, never a 1251 publication. [Sa'di](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/sadi-sirazi/), [Golestan](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/golestan-e-sadi/) |
| **32 · Continuity — 1501 CE** | **Ismail's accession at Tabriz and declaration of Shiism as the state's official faith.** A firm event about the construction of durable identity, including its coercive costs. Religious transformation was a process, not a completed national conversion that year. [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/safavids/) | **The Battle of Sharur in 1501 and the Qizilbash coalition behind Ismail.** Same firm year, but the emphasis becomes the military and personal dependencies underneath an apparently enduring identity. Same source. |
| **33 · Release — 1751 CE** | **Karim Khan's assumption of the regency in January 1751.** Power remains contested; this opens the question of ruling in another's name and holding authority in trust. His later “deputy of the people” title belongs after his establishment at Shiraz in 1765. [Karim Khan](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/karim-khan-zand/), [title chronology](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/wakil-al-raaya/) | **The installation of the nominal Safavid shah Ismail III in 1750.** A different viewpoint on the same transition: the inherited symbol survives while others wield the power. Useful if Release should expose possession disguised as stewardship. [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/karim-khan-zand/) |

## American / institutional track — Empire of Institution

The US remains the backbone. The 1876 and 1926 entries deliberately pair American and Canadian events; 1951 follows actual American diplomacy in Iran. A pair is a comparison unless the sources establish causation.

This is renewal as construction, with the exclusions of the founding still visible. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/lee-resolution), [Declaration timeline](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/timeline) | **Virginia's Declaration of Rights, 12 June 1776.** A more explicit design charter for legitimate government and rights; less immediately recognizable than the national founding. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/creating-the-united-states/peaceful-transition.html) | **The beginning of the war with Tripoli in 1801.** Agency under tribute, captivity and coercion; a stronger international confrontation, but less closely matched to the citizen's everyday dependence on systems. ## Recommended Indigenous thread

**Place the first small North American counterpoint in Chapter 25, beside the Persian 250 BCE condition.** By this period, communal bison hunting on the northern Plains already had a history measured in millennia. Head-Smashed-In in present-day southern Alberta supplies a geographically grounded example of ecological knowledge, coordinated work, food processing and material provision. Its archaeological history spans millennia and includes an interruption; we must not invent a hunt in the exact cutoff year or equate every ancient occupant with one present-day nation. [UNESCO's site assessment](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/158), [Jack Brink's archaeological account](https://read.aupress.ca/read/imagining-head-smashed-in/section/5b62a218-84bd-42b1-b9df-f8bbd348afca).

Two or three sentences are enough there. It supplies another economic possibility alongside imperial succession, while keeping the principal Persian anchor intact.

The proposed thread then runs:

- **Chapter 14 → Chapter 25:** recover an example of the ecological and institutional knowledge the earlier chapter says can be buried or erased. It should carry an actual mechanism, not merely an attractive image of harmony.
- **Chapter 27:** the 1876 US–Canada comparison shows what happens when institutions impose control over land, membership and livelihoods. Distinguish different nations, policies and periods; do not collapse all later violence into the original Indian Act.
- **Chapter 31, optionally:** choose the 1976 Indian Health Care Improvement Act if the author wants the health thread to continue explicitly here.
- **Chapter 32:** explore cultural continuity through living languages, teaching, land relationships and community authority over how knowledge is used. Indigenous peoples remain contemporary authors of their futures.
- **Chapter 33:** ask whether technological abundance can expand the practical freedom to choose different relationships to work, place, community and time.

The **Buffalo Treaty**, first signed in 2014 by Indigenous nations across the Canada–US border, is a useful present-day grounding for later future scenes. Its own account and articles provide an Indigenous-led example to learn from; it is not another historical cutoff or a claimed achievement of AI. [Treaty history](https://www.buffalotreaty.com/buffalo-treaty-history-and-origins), [articles](https://www.buffalotreaty.com/treaty).

**Alternative placement:** alongside the Persian 1751 entry, briefly mention the **1752 Peace and Friendship Treaty signed by Jean-Baptiste Cope and other Mi'kmaq delegates**, with its express hunting-and-fishing provision. This is a much tighter named event, one year after the cutoff. It belongs to particular signatories and communities, not all Indigenous peoples. I prefer the earlier Alberta seed because it gives the thread time to develop, but the treaty is a strong alternative for Release. [Treaty text](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100029040/1581293867988), [context and limits of participation](https://www.rcaanc-cirnac.gc.ca/eng/1100100028599/1539609517566).

The author's proposed meditation is about recovering possible ways of living as compulsory drudgery recedes. It need not assert that all Indigenous peoples were hunter-gatherers, that foraging was effortless, or that one social form universally fits human evolutionary psychology. The defensible design question concerns greater autonomy, time, reciprocity and ecological competence, with communities choosing their own futures. Harari can supply a provocation; specific biological and historical claims will need their own evidence when that passage is drafted.

## Decisions with the most consequence for the next pass

1. **501 / Making:** prefer the Kavad–Amida–resettlement cluster over the earlier broad Mazdak framing.
2. **1251 / Care:** retain the tighter Arghun Aqa anchor by default; Sa'di is the strongest emotionally motivated exception to consider.
3. **1926 / Governance:** keep King–Byng and add Myers to establish an explicit US comparison.
4. **1976 / Care:** swine flu remains the default; the Indian Health Care Improvement Act is a serious alternative.
5. **2026 / Release:** prefer the American anniversary, with Carney available as a short Canadian counterpoint.
6. **Indigenous seed:** prefer the Alberta bison economy alongside 250 BCE; the 1752 Mi'kmaq treaty is an alternative placement.

These are choices for author review, not instructions to insert every option or to give every chapter an identical two-scene historical formula. Interlude science/technology choices remain a separate discretionary selection. No chapter shuffle is proposed.
