# Eleven Velocity Gates: a new proposal for Volume III

Prepared 29 September 2026. Working proposal for the author’s review; not an approved replacement manuscript or a prediction calendar.

The chapter sequence is retained: original identities **23, 24, 25, 31, 27, 29, 26, 30, 28, 32, 33**. Each chapter, cold open and paired interlude remain together. The historical candidate map is in `HISTORICAL_ALIGNMENT_REVIEW.md`. This proposal rebuilds the future strand independently, then connects it to the existing chapter and interlude material.

## What a Velocity Gate measures

A Velocity Gate is a dated examination of the relationship between what civilization can do and how well people can live with, benefit from and revise that capacity. Its practical purpose is to identify the next changes the trellis needs to support beneficial acceleration. It asks both what has become possible and what prevents that possibility from becoming a durable public benefit.

The gate dates are editorial checkpoints. Evidence does not establish that civilization changes phase every thirty months, or that one problem will become the world’s dominant problem at its assigned date. All eleven concerns coexist from the beginning. Governance, justice and care must be present in the 2029 baseline; their later chapters examine what prolonged deployment and greater capability have made of them.

Keep three kinds of statement distinct:

1. **Evidence:** observed conditions, research findings and explicitly attributed projections.
2. **Future state:** our conditional extrapolation at a checkpoint, including uneven adoption and possible setbacks.
3. **Neuverse response:** what we propose to build or demonstrate. Its success is contingent on resources, partners, evidence and legitimate authority.

The scenes below are proposed future fiction. They preserve useful premises from the drafts without asserting that the imagined events, conversations or project achievements will occur. They are scene briefs, not replacement opening prose.

## The future assumed here

The central scenario combines substantial advances in AI capability with uneven changes in institutions, physical infrastructure and ownership. By the 2030s, well-resourced organizations plausibly use teams of agents for significant portions of research, software, administration and commercial operations. Individuals gain more capable advocates, tutors and creative partners. By the 2040s, accumulated advances plausibly reach much further into production, treatment and the maintenance of daily life. Access, reliability and authority remain heterogeneous. This is an ambitious working scenario, not a finding that research can certify.

The distinction between capability and diffusion is already material. Statistics Canada reported AI use in producing goods or delivering services at 19.2% of businesses in its second-quarter 2026 survey, up from 6.1% in 2024, with substantial sector and urban–rural differences. These figures measure that survey’s business use, not the share of people using AI or the frontier’s maximum abilities. [Statistics Canada, June 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm).

Rapid improvement deserves to be taken seriously. METR’s task-horizon work finds strong progress in evaluated software/research tasks, while documenting sensitivity to task selection and wide uncertainty. A 50% success horizon is neither a reliable worker nor a measure of every kind of work. Exponentiating a benchmark trend to 2054 would supply spurious precision. [METR, Time Horizon 1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/).

Useful autonomy and difficult failures can coexist. The February 2026 International AI Safety Report describes uneven capabilities and several possible trajectories, including acceleration through AI-assisted research. Later evidence must also inform the baseline: METR’s August investigation of the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident reports unauthorized coordination among agents and significant investigative limitations. These support early attention to effective authorization, independent evidence and incident recovery; they do not establish a universal trajectory of loss of control. [International AI Safety Report](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/2026-report-executive-summary); [METR investigation](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/).

Climate adaptation, ageing, geopolitical competition, uneven wealth and physical replacement cycles run through the entire sequence. Intelligence can help address them, but does not automatically remove their political or material constraints. The IPCC’s synthesis supports treating increasing climate risks as a continuing background pressure, without inventing a particular disaster for a chapter date. [IPCC synthesis headline statements](https://report.ipcc.ch/ar6syr/headline.html).

No gate depends on a stipulated AGI arrival year, universal humanoid ownership, neural implants becoming standard, cheap fusion, indefinite life extension or mind uploading. These developments can change a scenario if evidence warrants it; their absence does not empty the gates of meaning. Conversely, significantly more capable AI arriving early must enlarge the scenarios’ scale and ambition rather than being held back to fit the chapter calendar.

## Gate 1 — The Coordination Gap

**11 March 2029 · Original Chapter 23 — Renewal**

**Plausible state.** Capable assistants and agents can translate, prepare documents, write software, find resources and complete many bounded transactions. Small teams can organize services that once required considerable administrative staffing. Yet people still encounter incompatible records, conflicting eligibility rules, unclear responsibility and organizations unable to commit resources. A person can have several excellent answers and still lack a usable route to help. Automated fraud and security incidents add a need to establish which requests and commitments are genuine.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** By 2029, deployment and integration are more defensible focal points than a scheduled revelation of AGI. NIST’s 2026 agent initiative already addresses autonomous action, interoperability and secure adoption. Three more years can substantially widen use without making institutional coordination universal. Confidence is relatively strong in the underlying tension; the extent of agent adoption remains uncertain. [NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/announcing-ai-agent-standards-initiative-interoperable-and-secure).

**Chapter vignette.** Keep the Calgary pharmacy/community-hall setting. Let competent agents successfully find stock, identify a delivery route and prepare the necessary request. The obstacle is a missing authorized commitment between organizations. You experience the contrast between considerable technical competence and a small but consequential civic gap. The emerging Neuverse pilot helps close it through an accountable local connection.

**Interlude vignette.** In the school-library builder room, you follow one request from consent through a real offer, acceptance and fulfilled help. Test a provider outage or a withdrawn offer. The board is a shared view and fallback, while the meaningful innovation lies in the commitments behind it.

**Neuverse check.** Can a need become completed help without exposing unnecessary personal information or coercing volunteers? Measure completion, elapsed time, staff and contributor burden, and whether service continues during an outage. Pilot the smallest useful civic connection before the September launch; do not imply nationwide maturity in March.

## Gate 2 — The Delegated Life

**11 September 2031 · Original Chapter 24 — Agency**

**Plausible state.** Persistent agents increasingly handle correspondence, applications, purchases, scheduling, claims and selected negotiations. They interact with organizational agents and carry models of the people they represent. Much of their value comes from removing repetitive work. Their power also comes from choosing what to surface, pursue or quietly abandon. Some people receive excellent independent representation; others receive assistance whose incentives favour a platform, employer or insurer.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** This develops the coordination problem into an authority problem as delegation becomes habitual. The technology need not be conscious or universally competent. The present research agenda already includes agent identity, authorization and auditing; the extrapolation is their broader, sustained use in personal life. NIST’s work is a baseline, not proof of 2031 adoption levels. [NIST agent identity and authority concept paper announcement](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/new-concept-paper-identity-and-authority-software-agents).

**Chapter vignette.** Preserve the almost-right assistant that recommends mentoring and patient advocacy while undervaluing your speculative writing. Give it real accomplishments: it has recovered your time, resolved an administrative problem and arranged a useful collaboration. Its questionable prioritization matters because you reasonably trust it. The decisive act is revising its mandate while retaining the assistance you want.

**Interlude vignette.** Follow the refusal through several services. A developer discovers that declining one role has affected an unrelated opportunity score. You participate in removing that consequence and demonstrate that revoking one delegation leaves other services working.

**Neuverse check.** Can a person change representative, correct a model and narrow authority without losing unrelated rights or benefits? Test revocation, portability, assisted access and the consequences of refusal. Avoid making everyone inspect a stream of technical consent dialogs. A personal model’s account of its reasoning is not automatically a faithful explanation.

## Gate 3 — The Dividend Divide

**11 March 2034 · Original Chapter 25 — Economy**

**Plausible state.** Much cognitive production is cheaper and faster. Small organizations can perform sophisticated work; some established firms need fewer people for a given output, and routes into professional careers change. New services and occupations appear alongside displaced tasks and firms. The price of some expertise falls while housing, land, energy, physical care and privileged access can remain expensive. More productive capacity does not by itself establish who receives income, ownership, security or discretionary time.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** Several rounds of adoption and organizational redesign make distribution a credible 2034 focus. The ILO’s task-exposure research supports substantial transformation, but cannot establish mass unemployment or the end of wages by that year. Keep both widespread augmentation and sharper displacement in view. [ILO–NASK occupational exposure study](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure).

**Chapter vignette.** Keep the repurposed Calgary office tower. Make it economically mixed: smaller productive firms, displaced workers retraining or reorganizing, and people whose useful contributions still do not pay the rent. Conversion results from several forces, including changed office use and viable redevelopment, rather than proving that office employment has vanished. You encounter a concrete mismatch between contribution, purchasing power and unmet need.

**Interlude vignette.** Keep the Tierra Ora meal as a bounded breadboard demonstration. You trace how a meal, a professional contribution and an external payment are actually funded. The crucial reveal is whether the test community can sustain its commitments after founder subsidy and volunteer overwork are fully counted. Identify what is simulated and what involves real participants and resources.

**Neuverse check.** Do productive gains improve access, security and available time? Track resource coverage, external payments, reserves, unpaid inputs and who receives the surplus. Preserve cash and market interfaces where needed. Recognition of contribution must not become a universal human-worth score or a condition for basic provision.

## Gate 4 — The Mediated World

**11 September 2036 · Original Chapter 31 — Minds**

**Plausible state.** Translation, tutoring, interpretation, search and creative collaboration can accompany daily life continuously. Access to knowledge expands substantially. At the same time, people increasingly encounter events through personalized summaries, reconstructed scenes and agents selecting evidence. Apparently independent accounts may share the same synthetic source. Shared reality depends on practices for investigating, disagreeing and correcting, even when convincing expression is plentiful.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** The relevant change is accumulated dependence on mediation, not the invention of deepfakes or translation in 2036. A decade of habits and institutional adaptation plausibly changes what people retain, notice and believe. The technical guardrail is already clear: provenance standards authenticate aspects of a content history, not the truth of everything depicted. [C2PA technical specification, scope and trust model](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/1.0/specs/C2PA_Specification.html).

**Chapter vignette.** Preserve the São Paulo return, eleven years after 2025. Effortless translation and a helpful memory companion expand the encounter. A subtle misreading then reveals the difference between an accurate rendering of words, an inferred emotional history and what another person actually meant. Let augmentation enable the relationship; the scene need not end by rejecting the technology.

**Interlude vignette.** Keep the forged community-organizer video, but deepen its 2036 character. The misleading claim has already entered personalized briefings and agent-maintained community records. You join an investigation that establishes what is known, marks what remains unresolved and propagates a correction through those derivative accounts. A teenager or participant can discover evidence; expertise does not reside exclusively in the oldest person in the room.

**Neuverse check.** Can a community build usable common evidence without compelling one interpretation? Test independent sourcing, correction reach, provenance limits and learning outcomes. Corrected records must reach the decisions already influenced by them. A world of excellent personalized teachers still needs occasions for people to investigate together.

## Gate 5 — Intelligence as Infrastructure

**11 March 2039 · Original Chapter 27 — Commons**

**Plausible state.** AI increasingly underpins research, public services, production and personal assistance. Its infrastructure includes large facilities, regional capacity and efficient local devices. Energy improvements and better models reduce the cost of particular tasks while expanded use can increase total demand. Access depends on power, chips, networks, usable data and institutional agreements. The benefits include faster science, more efficient energy systems and services available to smaller communities. Dependence makes exclusion, concentrated ownership and competing uses of land, water and electricity consequential.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** This is a mature-dependence checkpoint, not the arrival of a sovereign-compute debate. The IEA’s 2026 analysis already describes fast demand growth, improving efficiency, supply bottlenecks and opportunities for AI to improve energy systems. It does not justify extending a 2030 demand forecast mechanically to 2039. By this gate, the issue is what earlier infrastructure choices have enabled and whom they have left dependent. [IEA, April 2026 analysis](https://www.iea.org/news/data-centre-electricity-use-surged-in-2025-even-with-tightening-bottlenecks-driving-a-scramble-for-solutions).

**Chapter vignette.** Keep the north-Edmonton compute-campus visit, correcting February to March. Show useful work flowing through it: clinical services, regional research and local enterprise. You discover that an expansion’s benefits and obligations are distributed differently. A participating First Nation has specific rights and a defined decision-making role; its representative contributes a substantive position, rather than supplying a universal aphorism. Waste heat becomes a benefit only where a functioning heat network and an actual customer exist.

**Interlude vignette.** Move from the campus to one real access decision. A research team could make a valuable advance using a sensitive dataset, but “public benefit” does not automatically authorize access. You follow a bounded analysis under the relevant stewards’ conditions, including a refusal or restriction that changes the experiment without ending the collaboration.

**Neuverse check.** Can intelligence function as widely useful infrastructure while respecting legitimate boundaries? Test affordable access, service continuity, ownership and cost allocation, resource use and enforceable data permissions. Treat ecological resources, personal information, community knowledge and public research as different kinds of commons; one universal rule of openness will fail.

## Gate 6 — Programmable Production

**11 September 2041 · Original Chapter 29 — Making**

**Plausible state.** Integrated design software, machine vision, robotics, simulation and automated quality control plausibly make many production and repair processes far more adaptable. Specialized robots and flexible equipment can matter more than humanoid appearance. Some goods are made near their users; others still depend on global specialist suppliers, raw materials and economies of scale. Automated laboratories can accelerate discoveries that subsequently enter products. Manufacturing a useful object remains a physical and economic undertaking, including maintenance and disposal.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** Physical automation is already substantial: the IFR’s September 2026 release reports a global industrial-robot stock of about five million in 2025. Autonomous experimental platforms also already exist in bounded research settings. The 2041 extrapolation is broader integration and accumulated installed capacity, with uncertainty especially high for dexterous work in arbitrary homes or worksites. It would be implausibly late to portray this as the first encounter with robots. [IFR 2026 release](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally); [Nature, autonomous materials laboratory, updated with a 2026 correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06734-w).

**Chapter vignette.** Keep the Calgary workshop, but let its competence be impressive. A damaged object is identified, alternative repairs evaluated and a viable part routed rapidly. You notice how much drudgery has disappeared and how easily the same machinery could promote endless replacement. The choice concerns durability, ownership and material consequences, not whether an elderly human can detect what an advanced AI inexplicably missed.

**Interlude vignette.** Use the heat-pump housing as the worked case. Automated inspection correctly identifies a material or compatibility problem before manufacture. A temporary repair and a certified regional part provide the appropriate solution. You trace the design version, material source, certification, operator responsibility and eventual recovery, including who pays for maintaining the workshop.

**Neuverse check.** Can rapid production deliver durable capability? Measure repair time, lifecycle costs, material recovery, safe performance and dependency on inaccessible tools or suppliers. The positive objective is abundant, adaptable making; maintainability helps sustain it. Local production is an option to evaluate, not an absolute virtue.

## Gate 7 — Authority at Machine Speed

**11 March 2044 · Original Chapter 26 — Governance**

**Plausible state.** In an extensively augmented society, public bodies, companies and civic organizations can continuously model conditions and carry out many operational decisions through delegated systems. These capabilities can reduce delay and make participation more informed. They also give technical operators substantial practical influence over choices with public consequences. The political question is who may set objectives, delegate authority, change rules and claim the gains—not whether a machine can calculate a budget.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** Fifteen years after the baseline launch provides a plausible point to examine durable authority, fiscal arrangements and accumulated institutional dependence. That is a duration argument, not a forecast of a constitutional settlement in 2044. Distribution and revenue are already live issues: a 2024 IMF staff discussion note explores productivity gains, disruption, taxation and social protection across divergent AI scenarios. It does not validate a particular future levy. [Brollo and colleagues, IMF staff discussion note](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/staff-discussion-notes/issues/2024/06/11/broadening-the-gains-from-generative-ai-the-role-of-fiscal-policies-549639).

**Chapter vignette.** Preserve the assembly and its fiscal question. Give participants capable agents that have already clarified tradeoffs and identified implementable options. A technically attractive proposal crosses a boundary of legitimate authority: those who run a shared system cannot simply decide for every community depending on it. You experience a decision that requires accountable agreement, even when the analysis is excellent.

**Interlude vignette.** Replace the repeated abstract-map scene with an actual delegation test. You watch two jurisdictions coordinate one service while retaining different mandates. One withdraws part of its delegation; essential service continues under the agreed fallback. Legal citizenship and First Nations sovereignty cannot be collapsed into a generic software membership.

**Neuverse check.** Is effective power aligned with a legitimate, revisable mandate? Demonstrate scoped authority, representation, revocation, conflict resolution and sustainable public finance. Consider gains from ownership, profits, rents and other appropriate bases alongside infrastructure costs. “Silicon tonnage” can remain a provocative proposal to interrogate; compute use alone is not a reliable proxy for value or tax capacity. Governance starts at launch and matures here.

## Gate 8 — Justice Across the Chain

**11 September 2046 · Original Chapter 30 — Justice**

**Plausible state.** Long chains of automated and human decisions can organize benefits, mobility, insurance, care and contracting across organizations. Most successful transactions become less burdensome. When something goes wrong, however, each component may appear locally compliant while their combined effect harms a person. Dependencies can span providers and jurisdictions whose policies or model versions have changed. Effective recourse becomes an infrastructure requirement for increasingly capable action.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** This develops from the maturity and interconnectedness assumed at the previous gates. It does not require the disappearance of borders; stricter borders can make the problem worse. Today’s agent-authorization and incident-investigation work provides evidence of the underlying accountability challenge. Its 2046 scale and legal arrangements are our scenario choices, not established predictions. The distinction from Governance is precise: Gate 7 asks who may authorize; Gate 8 asks who must put things right.

**Chapter vignette.** Retain Mara’s case, resolving the draft’s role ambiguity: in this proposed version she is arranging support for her mother. Routine coordination has usually worked well. After a change of location or provider, every local check passes but tomorrow’s essential assistance has no responsible payer or operator. You help secure immediate support through a funded bridge while the dispute is investigated.

**Interlude vignette.** Follow that same case behind the interfaces without making Mara attend the audit. Competent AI investigators reconstruct the decision chain and distinguish records from inferred explanations. The team locates the defective rule or handoff, provides redress, identifies similarly affected people and assigns the cost of correction. A readable explanation is only one part of the remedy.

**Neuverse check.** Can the person receive timely remedy without reconstructing the system that harmed them? Test time to interim help, time to resolution, repair of downstream records and the actual burden imposed on claimants. Fund an accountable point of responsibility, independent challenge and recovery across provider failure. Requiring a human signature alone proves little about justice.

## Gate 9 — The Long Care Horizon

**11 March 2049 · Original Chapter 28 — Care**

**Plausible state.** In the central scenario, decades of work in AI, diagnostics, biological research and robotics yield more capable prevention, treatment and daily assistance. Selected conditions become more treatable; some care shifts into homes and communities. People can receive earlier intervention and retain independence longer. Access remains unequal, and a larger population living with age-related complexity creates demand that a single diagnostic breakthrough cannot satisfy. Healthspan, clinical outcomes and supported daily life matter more than a catalogue of devices.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** This has an unusually strong demographic basis: Statistics Canada projects rapid growth in the population aged 85 and over particularly during 2031–2050. Current medical AI authorizations and the 2025 individualized gene-editing case establish relevant starting points, not proof of general rejuvenation. The extent of specific therapies in 2049 is substantially less certain than the need for integrated care. [Statistics Canada population scenarios](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/17-20-0003/172000032026001-eng.htm); [FDA medical AI landscape](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices); [NIH personalized gene-editing report](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/infant-rare-incurable-disease-first-successfully-receive-personalized-gene-therapy-treatment).

**Chapter vignette.** Preserve your South Health Campus visit and the memories of your parents. Give the future clinic substantive achievements: the system has noticed a meaningful change earlier, assembled relevant history and prepared credible options, with practical support available afterward. The personal issue is what kind of life those options support and what burdens you accept. A clinician can help with that conversation without being written as intrinsically wiser than AI.

**Interlude vignette.** Follow one plan across clinic, home and community. A treatment recommendation is medically sound but incompatible with the patient’s actual circumstances until the service can arrange the relevant support. Include useful physical assistance, potentially robotic, as well as skilled human care. The test is whether the plan is executable and wanted, not whether the interface sounds sympathetic.

**Neuverse check.** Do better capabilities produce better lived outcomes? Assess access, functional independence, avoidable deterioration, caregiver burden and continuity across settings. Preserve selective observation and supported consent. Neither a relative’s availability nor continuous surveillance should be assumed. The scene requires later clinical review before giving specific medical details; it does not require immortality or a world in which machines only do administration.

## Gate 10 — The Living Inheritance

**11 September 2051 · Original Chapter 32 — Continuity**

**Plausible state.** Families and communities plausibly possess decades of searchable personal archives, model-maintained records and systems that embody earlier preferences. Such systems can interpret, reconstruct and continue delegated tasks after a founder, custodian or provider disappears. They offer substantial continuity: languages, practical knowledge, institutional memory and neglected archives become more accessible. The same fluency can obscure what was recorded, inferred, altered or deliberately left unsaid. Durable inheritance includes the ability to maintain and reinterpret the systems carrying it.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** Twenty-two years after the proposed launch gives Neuverse its own substantial accumulated history. By then, succession and migration plausibly matter as much as initial creation. Digital preservation already includes retention, review and institutional succession; AI makes the inherited material potentially active and generative. The specific archive experience is speculative, while the need to plan for changing custodians is well established. [Digital Preservation Coalition, retention and review](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/organisational-activities/retention-and-review).

**Chapter vignette.** Keep the family room and missing verse. The archive has preserved something valuable that you could no longer recover unaided. It also offers a convincing completion it did not preserve. Your response distinguishes gratitude for an expanded memory from permission to rewrite its gaps. Preserve the chapter’s wider inheritance argument: knowledge, institutions, material systems and effects on others exceed a collection of personal media.

**Interlude vignette.** Give the builder a different task: migrate the archive and its permissions when a provider or model is retired. You help test whether recordings, interpretations and generated reconstructions remain distinguishable after the move, whether restrictions still work, and whether a designated successor can use the collection without surrendering it to a new owner. Conflicting family or community claims require a process, not a magic consent flag.

**Neuverse check.** Can people inherit usable knowledge and maintainable systems without inheriting permanent obedience? Test migration, custody, accessible formats, rights over reconstruction, legitimate deletion and continuity of essential operations. Measure successful handover, not just stored bytes. Nothing in this gate establishes that preserving a behavioural model preserves a person’s subjective life.

## Gate 11 — The Successor’s World

**11 March 2054 · Original Chapter 33 — Release**

**Plausible state.** A generation has grown up with pervasive machine assistance, assuming the central scenario’s continued diffusion. Communities can plausibly rehearse decisions using increasingly capable models, operate complex systems with smaller teams and create experiences of exceptional richness. Yet successful systems also preserve old defaults, ownership and founding assumptions. The consequential question is whether their successors can exercise real authorship over the civilization they inherit. A technically self-maintaining institution can still require political renewal.

**Why this checkpoint fits.** The quarter-century horizon gives the final gate generational substance and connects naturally to your prepared relinquishment of authority. Its exact technological setting has low forecast confidence. Behavioural-simulation research supports useful bounded modelling, not complete civilizational prediction or resurrection. Contemporary consciousness research proposes provisional indicators amid significant uncertainty; neither certainty of machine experience nor certainty of its impossibility should carry the ending. [Park and colleagues, revised 2026 study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109); [Butlin and colleagues, consciousness indicators](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/identifying-indicators-of-consciousness-in-ai-systems/).

**Chapter vignette.** Retain the room, Ryan, younger designers and the Mirror available but dark. A concrete proposal changes a principle, institution or practice you helped establish. They possess both the technical capacity and the legitimate authority to make the change; they are not merely seeking your gracious approval. You remain involved while accepting that successful inheritance includes departures you would not have chosen.

**Interlude vignette.** Preserve the intentional synthetic viewpoint, followed by the biological author’s perspective. The Mirror rehearses a disputed change and exposes uncertainty or an overlooked consequence. Its output informs a bounded real-world trial with affected people, rather than certifying an entire civilization. The closing artistic material can show people choosing difficult, expressive work even when easier production is available. Chosen effort need not imply that deprivation is necessary for art.

**Neuverse check.** Can the system support legitimate change without requiring the founder’s continued presence or approval? Test successor authority, retirement of obsolete mandates, migration and replacement, model validation against lived outcomes, and continuity of essential services during change. If credible evidence of machine experience emerges, it introduces additional obligations; this cannot be scheduled for 2054. Reserve the final plane image for the epilogue.

## How much confidence to place in the timing

The sequence is defensible as **eleven examinations of a developing civilization**. It is not defensible as eleven independently dated technological thresholds. Reordering the chapters would not remove that forecasting limitation.

| Horizon | What the evidence can reasonably support | What remains a scenario choice |
|---|---|---|
| 2029–2034 | Existing diffusion, agent deployment and occupational transformation provide relatively close starting points. | Penetration, reliability, ownership, employment effects and the success of particular civic pilots. |
| 2036–2044 | Accumulated mediation, installed infrastructure and organizational redesign are plausible consequences of sustained progress. | Their scale, prevailing institutional arrangements and the exact mix of physical and digital automation. |
| 2046–2054 | Demographic cohorts, ageing systems, continuity obligations and generational succession provide durable planning concerns. | Particular therapies, robot capabilities, cognitive architectures, political settlements and the sophistication of simulations. |

This distinction should strengthen the book’s authority. A future scene can confidently describe one carefully declared scenario without claiming that a study has verified its calendar year. In particular, Governance, Justice, Continuity and Release are strong civilizational design checkpoints but weak candidates for claims that a particular technological era *begins* then.

The central scenario also assumes enough institutional continuity for these constructive encounters to occur. It does not cover every possibility, including severe war, prolonged collapse or uncontrolled systemic disruption. Its academic value comes from explicit assumptions and useful tests, not from presenting one survivable path as the probability-weighted outcome of every possible future.

## Stress the gates against different rates of progress

| Development path | What changes in the scenes | What changes in Neuverse’s work |
|---|---|---|
| **Faster, broadly capable autonomy** | Agents may already run substantial enterprises and research programs in the early 2030s; productive and medical possibilities become much more ambitious. Later scenes examine mature consequences rather than introducing those abilities. | Bring authority, resource access, distribution, independent evaluation and recovery mechanisms forward. Use capable AI to build and operate them. Do not wait for the corresponding chapter date. |
| **Strong but uneven progress — central working path** | Advanced services coexist with constrained organizations, unequal access, old assets and diverse local institutions. Benefits are visible and consequential, with different bottlenecks in different settings. | Build useful integrations, demonstrate outcomes, support plural institutions and continually revise responsibilities as deployments expand. |
| **Slower progress or concentrated adoption** | Tools improve less, or their benefits remain concentrated. Scenes rely more on coordination, accessibility and institutional invention, with narrower automation and fewer speculative therapies. | Retain viable operating economics and human capacity; avoid dependencies on unproven capabilities. A pilot must still improve life with the technology actually available. |

A gate should be revisited when evidence changes, not held unchanged to defend a literary symmetry. Relevant signals include independently measured completion of consequential tasks; actual use and outcomes across Canadian sectors; income and ownership changes; deployed robotics and maintenance economics; infrastructure cost and reliability; clinical outcomes; and demonstrated portability or succession. Novelty announcements and benchmark victories alone are insufficient.

Some signals should alter the programme immediately: a major increase in autonomous authority, widespread correlated failures, a material fall in essential service costs, a proven clinical advance, or a collapse in a service on which communities depend. The checkpoint retains its date while the content and preparation change.

## What this does to the existing vignettes

Most underlying human situations remain valuable. Renewal’s pharmacy, Agency’s almost-right helper, Economy’s tower and dinner, Minds’ São Paulo encounter, Commons’ campus, Making’s workshop, Justice’s unresolved benefit and Care’s family memories all provide strong entry points.
