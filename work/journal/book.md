# Book-wide guidance and pending work

This is an author-editable journal. Standing guidance remains active; checkboxes are for work that can actually be completed.

## Standing guidance — adopted 30 September 2026

### B-001 — Three editorial passes

1. **Historical braid:** research, choose and weave the two past empire anchors and their consequences into each chapter's design argument. Use the accepted gate structure; verify facts rather than bending them to the pattern. Introduce interlude science/technology anchors selectively. Do not launch a broad condensation or vignette rewrite in this pass.
2. **Design and voice:** condense wasteful exposition and increase the amount of useful design knowledge per passage. Preserve and strengthen dark humor, wit, satire, technical language, unusual associations and the author's quirks. Cut repetition, atmospheric padding, empty explanation and generic polish. Preserve substantive intellectual, historical, scientific, biological, technological, methodological and systems/design content. No arbitrary percentage target and no removal of humor just to shorten the text.
3. **Future vignettes and bridges:** align scenes with the revised Velocity Gates and credible future conditions, then improve their transitions into the chapter. Protect real recollections. Make a possible future vivid without implying spurious forecast precision through a date announcement at every opening.

These are focused passes and may each require multiple steps. Before cross-volume callbacks are woven or changed, read the relevant earlier material. The author wants a full earlier-volume reading at a suitable stage, so Volume III can repay the context built by Volumes I and II. Do not claim that full reading has occurred yet.

### B-002 — Reader value and voice

The target is writing that is useful, intellectually substantial, funny, sometimes enigmatic and technically credible. The author's complaint is that too much current design prose delivers too little design knowledge. Tighten the work while preserving its expressive range. The style guide should recognize the author, not make him conform to generic literary polish. Let recurring images work by cadence and implication; do not explain hidden connections after they land.

### B-003 — Factual memory and projected scenes

The São Paulo and South Health Campus recollections are real and cannot be changed to suit a future plot. New future framing, dates and encounters may be redesigned around them, with a clear distinction between memory and conjecture. The Mirror in Interlude 33 and the Breadboard test carry the author's own ideas and should not be discarded as generic inherited LLM scenery.

Use Alberta, and occasionally Vancouver, as recurring home ground where useful. Other settings remain available. Vary the action and perspective so the volume does not become a future travelogue or a procession of men entering impressive rooms. Future capability should be measured by how life and systems work, not decorative gadgets.

### B-004 — Protect subtext

The author's father was a civil engineer. Overture III uses his aging body, engineering vocabulary and the phone offered to Ryan to stage a possible handoff from the old civilization/Carbon to its child or creation, Silicon. The register shifts toward computing at the transfer. The child's bow leaves open whether the successor will honor human culture or flourish beyond the aging bridge on the other bank. The river also carries a possible species-divergence association (the author's Congo image), not an empirical claim already established for publication.

The Epilogue's plane transformation and “flicker of my first light” carry an intended emotional/cadential relationship to the Oppenheimer/Gita association. Preserve that weight without inserting an explanatory allusion, borrowing a quotation or spelling out the symbolic machinery. The Overture-to-Epilogue relationship is editorial knowledge, not a paragraph to add to the book.
