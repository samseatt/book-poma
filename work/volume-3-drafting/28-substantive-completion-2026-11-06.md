## Charter grounding

The requested **8 Neufacture, Industry, Material Lifecycle Charter.md** was reviewed selectively: full orientation, lifecycle, advanced-industry, biological-production and operating-principles sections, plus substantive selections on material fit, organ relations, Glia, product twins, AI, stewardship, making at different scales and pilots. It is a long document, not uniformly read cover to cover. Exact scope and source hash are in the audit.

Neufacture is an industrial/material-lifecycle organ, not just a network of printers. Its useful loop is interpret need → design → source → materialize → deploy → maintain and recover → learn, with proportionate product memory throughout. Neuconomy supplies the context of need and consequence; Neubank helps provision and route; xGaia brings surrounding ecological conditions into decisions; Tiers supplies cooperating infrastructure. Clinical authority remains with the appropriate care pathway. Glia/microglia appear only where material memory needs explanation. TACK is a proposed organizing discipline, not a safety certification. No charter term is evidence of implemented capability.

The seven-tier and first-sowing context from pair 26 remains applicable. Neo4j is the current implementation direction: material relationships can contain cycles, while particular derivation histories remain ordered. No implication that the entire network must be a DAG, that an organ is merely a software service, or that every machine adapter is a Tier 6 Connector. No iNeural dependency.

The ecological requirement established with pair 27 is carried into the design itself: cumulative demand, watershed and seasonal conditions, soil functions, habitat, actual recovery and nonhuman value. A favorable cost or carbon estimate cannot cancel an unacceptable effect elsewhere. Equally, recyclability is not an unconditional veto on a safer, longer-lived material with a better whole-life outcome. Material choice requires a real comparison.
