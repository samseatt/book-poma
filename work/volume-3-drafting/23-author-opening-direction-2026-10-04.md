```text
Chapter 23: The Loom of Renewal: This is the part of other book where the arc of history bends permanently from the past into the future. When a specific date reveals itself as the horizon—a cosmic stop date—moving forward through time begins to feel like looking backward into history. When something starts and hasn't ended but you already start seeing its asymptotic curve - often called the **Singularity curve** in futurism - you can infer the boundary wall of that activation function. It cease to be unknown and becomes a probability machine. Not the wave going into the future, but a metaphorical wave function whose equation we plug reality into. Even here in 2029, we still do not know if this boundary signifies the final end of human civilization, or a gainful renewal with its own equations. The only thing with absolute mathematical certainty we can say now is that philosophy is at a 99.65% sigmoid asymptote of being dead. Every living philosopher—and yes, there is still such a thing—is talking about AI and nothing else.
So here I am in 2029, standing at this first gate of thought experiments. March 11 arrives exactly the way spring always arrives in Calgary: still sufficiently buried in the heavy snows of winter's past. And like a spring that simply forgot to unload, my mind too instantly drifts to the same dreadful day of my dad’s protocol-based execution three years ago. The desperate, futile *what-ifs* began to circle. What if I had done something differently? Reacted more forcefully? What if I had ripped the IV out and cut it into pieces so the nurses couldn't just slide it back in? What if I had started kicking and breaking things just to force someone to look at him deeper than instruction? If only I had spent more time with him in the year before the end, talking to him lovingly rather than always exhausting my spirit fighting the healthcare—or rather, healthcareless—machine.
But after three years of practice even emotional thoughts become efficient. By now time had fully indexed the entire saga into the thought of that day - like a Candy Man of some sort that could just be summoned by staring hard enough in the mirror of March 11.
Yet, as quickly as it came, this personal *what-if* veered into the larger arc of the world. To the global events of that same era: what if humanity had stood up that year? Had we done something different? The year 2026 was an ample and apt harbinger—infrastructure vulnerabilities, two major wars burning in the backdrop of agentic, nuclear, and biological warfare. We were losing personal agency to high tech; the internet was eating human social interaction alive. There was the constant, looming threat of top-down industrial automation, economic checkpoints, hegemony, ideology, climate crisis, the death of physical and data privacy, and—did I mention AI?
I am glad these thoughts, unpracticed as they were, came and went in what humans used to call a flash. Because before this Sunday morning could fully turn into a Sunday mourning, my agent—yes, we don't cheapen them by calling them "AI agents" anymore like we did back in 2026—just informs me that it had finished its "errands."
Wow. This is a massive development. I can't wait to read the completion summary—which is a merciful, gentle name for the humility of an agent summarizing its epic, incomprehensible tome of Neuralese into an easy-to-understand English brief. As if, I, the human "I," were a grand wizard and it just a humble wand. These results were critical. In a matter of minutes, it had accomplished what would have taken a highly intelligent human an entire year to achieve. Even better, if you go back in frames of quarter millennium, this kind of wizardry—condensed into a casual notification reading *"Worked for 12m 51s"*—could command you an entire empire, or better yet, a divine realism.
Anyway, here I am, looking at the results now. This particular query does not relate to my work—the project we sometimes playfully call the "breadboard," a term from a silly book I recently wrote to describe a concept I was trying to conceive back then, though I didn't fully nail it the way I have now. No, this file is looking at a specific reality completely outside that breadboard. Something raw. Something that affects me deeply outside my work...
```

## Editorial reading — recommendations, not adopted edits

The principal movement is worth developing: step forward from Theta's 2026 viewpoint into an explicitly imagined March 2029; return through the anniversary to the author's father; widen the counterfactual to civilization; let a competent agent interrupt with a personally consequential result. It gives the opening its own emotional cause and allows the narrated present to become immediate after one clear imaginative threshold. A future date alone does not establish conjecture; retain a short explicit act of imagining or stepping forward.

Preserve the particular strengths: grief acquiring efficiency through repetition; the narrator's anger and counterfactual helplessness; Sunday morning/mourning if it fits the final cadence; the inversion of human wizard and computational wand; enormous capability arriving as an ordinary duration stamp. Let humour implicate the narrator's importance, habits, and language. Grief need not deliver a punchline. The claim that philosophy is 99.65% dead reads as deliberately bogus precision; sharpen its satirical target rather than treating it as a statistic requiring proof.

The mathematical preamble needs conceptual selection and compression. A logistic sigmoid saturates toward a bounded limit; it does not exhibit a finite-date divergence. An asymptote, activation function, wave function, and technological singularity are not interchangeable models, and seeing acceleration does not reveal an empirically established date or a reliable equation for civilization's future. Preserve the imaginative horizon and retrospective-future effect without making an unearned scientific guarantee. The 99.65% joke can then remain conspicuously satirical. Also distinguish the narrator's forward-moving journey from the historical strand, which continues through all eleven chapters.

The local emotional transition carries more force than an exhaustive list of the world's troubles. Once Theta contains the fuller 2026 account, select a few concrete reminders rather than repeat its inventory. The wording about agentic, nuclear, and biological warfare must eventually distinguish observed events, capabilities, and feared escalation. No current-events certification was undertaken in this intake. March weather is a scene choice, not an assertion of reliable heavy snow every year.

Retain an ambitious 2029 agent. Make its accomplishment specific before presenting a year-to-minutes comparison; distinguish information processing and coordinated digital work from external investigations, validation, physical actions, or institutional outcomes it has not completed. A completion summary can be comically addressed to the supposedly commanding human without being described as a literal, faithful translation of all internal computation. Keep “Neuralese” visibly metaphorical if used that way.

Once the narrative enters 2029, use present tense for its immediate action and past tense for the actual 2026 recollection. Do not silently convert the March 11 initiating medical event into the father's death date. The phrase “protocol-based execution” is the author's charged characterization, not an independent finding established by this editorial intake. Preserve the author-supplied account; later factual/medical treatment must remain faithful to the source material.

The personal inquiry at the end remains open. Its result should lead promptly to something the author must face, choose, or do. The agent need not fail to create narrative tension: what it can now establish or accomplish can itself force the human question. Do not automatically make the task a retrospective medical verdict or a Breadboard demonstration. A brief Breadboard aside may orient the distinction, but the story should reach the personal result before explaining the project or its naming history.
