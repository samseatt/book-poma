### How to prevent duplication and dependency circles

- **23 versus 24:** a service commitment has a minimal permission boundary from the start; the full personal delegation/inference lifecycle belongs to 24.
- **24 versus 29:** a person's authorization is not an institution's jurisdiction. Explain individual control in 24, collective mandates and amendments in 29.
- **25 versus 27:** allocation and entitlement are economic choices; resource condition, replenishment, access and maintenance govern the commons. Price does not settle authority.
- **26 versus 32:** provenance helps assess a live claim in 26; preservation across formats, contexts and generations is the additional burden in 32.
- **27 versus 28:** commons arrangements govern shared productive resources; the workshop teaches how a specific object and process can actually be made, repaired and qualified.
- **29 versus 30:** authority determines who may decide; justice also asks whether the decision and its consequences can be challenged and repaired.
- **30 versus 31:** the care system must respond before harm where possible; it cannot wait for an appeal to supply ordinary clinical attention. Remedy remains available when care fails.
- **Θ versus 33:** every trial needs comparison, limitations and an exit from the start; 33 studies the harder problem of deployment, succession and simulated evidence at scale.

Early pairs use existing legitimate institutions and modest implementation choices. Pair 23 does not require a future currency from 25, a new constitution from 29 or a high-fidelity simulated civilization from 33. Later chapters specialize and sometimes revise the first arrangements. This is a learning sequence, not a construction schedule with missing foundations.

## The Breadboard's operating contract

The newly inserted prose establishes this contract. The fuller record is for editors and future builders; it is not a form to repeat eleven times in the book.

**Unit:** a specific useful service or intervention, with named participants, authority, resources and a time boundary. The community is larger than the model. A need, an offered capacity, an accepted commitment, a delivered service and a satisfied person are distinct states or judgments.

**Modes:** synthetic rehearsal → consenting bounded pilot → continuing operation, with a new evidentiary and governance burden at each transition. No success automatically promotes itself to the next mode. Clinical, public and cultural authority cannot be acquired from a generic participation agreement.

**Minimum evidence:** intended improvement; existing comparison; assumptions and exclusions; alternatives considered; resource and cost account; observed result; burden by participant group; exceptions, dissent and revision decision. Include useful options and time released, not only hazard counts. A small convenient sample cannot establish population-wide effects.

**Minimum mechanics:** accepted ownership at handoffs, bounded permission, resource reservation, correction/complaint route, failure backstop, non-digital access where needed, and an end to temporary powers. Pair 23 develops states and tests. Personal permission, finance and political authority acquire their fuller treatments later.

**A recurring test without eleven repeated scenes:** start with a journey or interpretation request. Later examine permission withdrawal, the funding claim, evidence correction, resource scarcity, a replacement component, competing mandates, a remedy, a clinically consequential handoff, custodial succession and founder departure. These need not all happen to one fictional family or inside one project. Use the relevant mechanism, not a compulsory plot.

**A meaningful failure:** the experiment can reveal that the bottleneck is lack of staff, money, vehicles or legitimate authority, rather than poor coordination. That finding must be allowed to change the proposal. Demonstrate successful assistance too; beneficial AI speed is part of the design, not a danger admitted only reluctantly.

**Vocabulary:** Neusphere names the aspiration; Neuverse is the author's proposed means of helping realize it; the Breadboard is a way to test arrangements. Tierra Ora may remain the economy interlude's bounded example. Its “dark energy” cannot stand in for an account of contributions, costs, recruitment and subsidy. Neither C4A's proposed expansion nor Speed/Light naming is globally settled here.
