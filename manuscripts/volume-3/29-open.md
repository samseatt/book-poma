_Good thoughts, good words, good deeds._  
--- **Zoroastrian creed** (*Humata, Hūkhta, Hvarshta*)

Peacham, Vermont, March 2044. Town Meeting Day, as it has been on the first Tuesday of March for more than two centuries: folding chairs in the school gym, a moderator with a gavel, pie in the back and mud season just beginning outside.

Article 9 on the warrant is the culvert on the Danville road. The town's assistant has done the homework for everyone: three options, the engineering, the costs over thirty years, the flood maps, a summary anyone can hear in their own words before the meeting opens. Most people have. The recommended option is sensible, and the voting should take ten minutes.

Then Ruth Hale, who has farmed below that culvert for fifty years, stands up and asks to amend. The sensible option, she says, sends the spring melt straight onto the Wheelers' lower field. She knows, because it went there in '29, and in '37, and the flood maps were drawn by people who weren't standing in it.

The assistant checks while she's still talking. She's right. The maps missed it.

The moderator asks whether there's a second. There are about forty.

The amended article passes. It costs a little more. Nobody minds.

On the way out, a girl voting for the first time asks her father why they didn't just let the machine decide.

"It did fine," he says. "It just hadn't met Ruth."
