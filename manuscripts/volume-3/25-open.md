_He who eateth what he hath earned by his toil, and giveth some of it away, O Nanak, he knoweth the true way._  
--- after **Guru Nanak**, _Guru Granth Sahib_ 1245

Belém, March 2034. The rains have come early to the mouth of the Amazon, and the market at Ver-o-Peso smells of river, fish and wet cardboard, as it has for three hundred years.

Dona Graça has sold açaí from the same stall since before her granddaughter was born. These days the granddaughter's agents do most of the selling: to cafés in São Paulo, a cooperative in Lisbon, a hotel chain that wants "provenance" printed on its menus. The orders arrive overnight, the boats are booked before dawn and the money lands before the fruit does. Last year, the stall earned more than it had in the previous ten.

The granddaughter shows her the numbers on a screen. Dona Graça nods, the way she nods at weather.

Then she fills the first bowl of the morning, thick and purple, and hands it across the counter to the boy from the next stall, whose mother is sick and whose father is out on the river.

"Vovó, that's not on the order."

"It's never been on the order," says Dona Graça, and fills the second.

---

*What is abundance for, if not the bowl that was never on the order?*
