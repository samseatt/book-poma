_He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?_  
--- **Micah 6:8**

Lagos, September 2046. The rains are ending, and Mama Ronke has received a letter she can't read the first time through.

The state's new land registry, which has spent the year turning a century of paper into a single record, has marked the title to her house as *unverified*. The deed her late husband registered in 1994 is missing from the archive. Under the old system, a word like that would have been followed by a man with a clipboard, then a man with a bulldozer.

Her grandson reads the letter again, slowly, out loud. The second page is different. The registry has found that the 1994 ledgers for her whole ward were lost in a flood, and that four hundred and twelve titles were flagged for the same reason. None of them will be acted on. Each has been restored to its prior standing while the registry rebuilds the missing pages from survey records, neighbors' deeds and the old men who remember where every boundary stone was set. Nobody has to appear anywhere. Nobody has to prove they exist.

Mama Ronke folds the letter and puts it under the clock on the shelf, where her husband kept everything important.

"So they made the mistake," she says, "and they're the ones fixing it."

"Yes, Mama."

She considers this for a long time.

"Then they've learned something," she says. "Tell them I said so."
